# World first: the five rounds (mission worldcrafter-gwx3)

Sumeet's direction of 2026-11-05 (decision worldcrafter-jeue, strategy
`docs/strategies/world-demo.md`): a demonstration of the Demiurge-assisted
process, not a comparative eval. Round 0 fixed the tool; then five rounds,
each with a fresh Opus 5.5 writer on the same premise ("Wait. Let me get this
straight. You are a warlock that doesn't have a single patron, but hundreds
of lesser patrons all watching you at the same time? And... you call them
'Chat'?"), four scenes at points the orchestrator chose, and a panel of four
judges (Opus 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Fable 5.1, Grok 4.7) each reading one scene and
its story so far against `docs/verdicts/world-demo-rubric.md`. After each
round a separate reader audited the writer's full transcript and model, and
the fixes were reviewed and merged before the next round.

Round logs: [round-1.md](round-1.md), [round-2.md](round-2.md),
[round-3.md](round-3.md), [round-4.md](round-4.md), [round-5.md](round-5.md).
Each round's scenes, stories so far, scores, outline and reading are copied
in `round-<n>/`.

## World aliveness: the thesis signals

The thesis was that a rich, full world makes the story feel more alive. What
the judges cited under world aliveness, in all 80 reports:

- **Seen in every round:** institutions and economies glimpsed in passing
  rather than explained: "The bounty is nine crowns. Less the reeve's tenth"
  (Round 1); "the chemist wants six crowns on Friday" (Round 2); "Under the
  terms an unheld seat is struck at the next audit" (Round 3); "Old Vessen
  stage-silk … Nobody's imported it in forty years" (Round 4); "A god whose
  house is pledged is a thing that is owed" (Round 5). Implied history and
  consequence: "the face rubbed off by sixty years of thumbs", "a ferry-god
  … the bridge that put him out of work", "the bell of Saint Aldo that had
  gone into the cannon foundry". Small gods with distinct, specific
  textures (the Nine Wet Gentlemen, a milestone god "lean and lean and then
  give").
- **Not seen:** no score moved with the tool's changes. World aliveness
  never fell below 7 and reached 9 in five of 80 reports: once in Round 1
  and four times in Round 4 (three of them on Round 4's first scene). Round
  4's bible had the most facts and actors (53 and 34) and Round 5's the
  fewest facts (27), but the per-judge means don't line up with bible size
  in the other rounds, and five stories are too few to say. Judges never
  cited exposition as world aliveness, as the rubric asked.

## What the tool and skill changes did

From the five readings (`round-<n>/reading.md`; Round 5's closes with a
section across all five rounds):

| | Round 1 | Round 2 | Round 3 | Round 4 | Round 5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| CLI output | silenced by the writer after trace noise | all seen | all seen | all seen | all seen |
| Pre-scene read | `show` only | reader context cut by `tail` | read whole in 2 of 4 | whole in 4 of 4 | whole in 4 of 4; source of every opening number |
| Numbers checked after drafting | none possible | none | all 4 scenes | 2 of 4 | 3 of 4 |
| Traces acted on | none seen | 0 of 8 | 0 of 24 suspects real | 1 real, acted on | 1 block, noise |
| Viewpoint marks | none | none | 11 of 12 false late | about half false late | 8 of 8 true for the main viewpoint |
| Continuity errors in judged prose | 4 (two counts, two clocks, a day count) | 2 (unchecked numbers) | 1 meaning error, 1 misplaced line | 1 object state on a retold night | none confirmed |

In the readings, each class of continuity error went away in the round
after its fix and didn't come back: count arithmetic (counts), two clocks
(memberships), what a number counts (skill), misplaced lines (skill). One
qualification: Round 5's reading found `span`'s day counts off by one between
whole-day events, latent since Round 2 (17 of 144 on Round 2's model, 47 of
186 on Round 3's, 39 of 119 on Round 5's). The readings' "day counts all
right" checks for Rounds 2–5 used the same function and are therefore
unverified; worldcrafter-yxfp re-checks the day counts stated in those
rounds' judged prose. The judges' continuity
scores don't show this: the panel read one scene at a time against a short
story so far, and noticed one of the logged errors in five rounds.
