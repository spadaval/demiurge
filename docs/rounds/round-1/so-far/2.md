Jess is a warlock, and the first time we meet her she's waist-deep in Marrow Fen, fighting a bog-wight for a bounty of nine crowns. She talks the whole way through the fight, describing every move out loud to an audience nobody else can see. That audience is Chat. Gristlemouth wants her to bite the thing. Mothlight is grading her form. The Widow fusses over her. Brindle says hello to her first, as he always does. Twelve-Pence is busy counting the bounty. When the fight turns, Jess calls a poll, punch or fire, and Chat votes for fire. Power floods into her, the wight burns, and then she slips and falls into the peat anyway. Her magic runs on tithes, polls and backlash, and it answers to many voices at once. Someone has been watching from the reeds: a woman in the grey of a Vigilant, who sees a warlock thank an empty fen.

That woman is Odile. She finds Jess at the village inn over a bowl of eel stew and arrests her strictly by the book, reading the warrant out in full. Jess has never been arrested by anyone so polite. In the lockup she explains how her power works. She has no single great patron. She has hundreds of small ones, and all of them are watching right now. Odile repeats it back slowly, trying to get it straight: hundreds of lesser patrons, all watching at once, and Jess calls them "Chat"? Yes, says Jess, and she compares them to the gallery at a playhouse. Meanwhile Chat's reactions to Odile keep scrolling past. They adore her, and Mothlight wants to run a poll on her eyebrows. Jess tells the story she always tells about how this started. She was an actress, she forgot her lines on a stage in Saltreach, and she made one up. Odile writes it all down as evidence.

Now the two of them are on the road to Aurenne, chained wrist to wrist. Jess can't stop talking, and that's not just habit: she literally can't. She narrates breakfast, the weather and Odile's horse. At night Odile prays to a god that has never answered her. Silence, she says, is also an answer.

Somewhere along the way Jess notices that Brindle hasn't said good morning in two days. When she presses Twelve-Pence, they're evasive, but they admit that several regulars have gone quiet this spring. Jess does what she always does and turns it into a bit. She holds a mock funeral for a dog-spirit, and Chat laughs. Then she lies awake.
