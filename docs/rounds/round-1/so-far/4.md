Jess is a warlock with no single great patron. Instead she has hundreds of small ones, minor gods and spirits watching her all the time, and she calls them Chat. She talks to them constantly. She runs polls and they vote, and their regard gives her power, at a price. Some are regulars: Gristlemouth, who always wants a fight; Mothlight, who rates her form; the Widow, who fusses over her; Brindle the dog-spirit; Twelve-Pence, who counts the money; and Old Sedge, who sleeps.

A quiet, rule-following Vigilant named Odile arrests her in a fen village and takes her in chains toward Aurenne. On the road Jess notices that Brindle has gone silent, and Twelve-Pence admits that other regulars have too. At the Sedge ferry they're attacked by Lucan Thrale, someone from Jess's past. He tells her the Ledger, a power that collects and owns, is buying her piece by piece. "You said a line that wasn't in the script, Jess." The ferryman Abel Drury pulls them out of the river.

A Notary reads out the Register of Jess's pact. The Ledger holds a growing share. A large number of parts are "dormant", and the biggest single part belongs to a silent Witness. Jess made the pact years ago on the stage of the Gilded Lamp in Saltreach. She went blank in the middle of a play and a line came to her from nowhere. Odile was secretly in the audience that night, a runaway novice of fifteen. She doesn't say so, but she unchains Jess.

Mothlight sells his part in the middle of a fight at Hask Bridge and many others flee after him. The Widow saves Jess and is badly weakened, and Odile kills a man for the first time. Brother Cray, carrying an irregular order to kill Jess, keeps hunting them. The Register reveals that the dormant parts belong to the people who were in the theatre that night. Whoever holds more than half can call the pact. Jess and Odile track down Quell, Jess's old company master. He admits he promised her to a devil and hands over the house book. Odile finds that audience members are being murdered so the Ledger can take their parts. Mags Tolley, a baker, wakes her part and becomes the first mortal to speak in chat.

The Witness turns out to be the Unblinking, Odile's silent god, which has been watching Jess through Odile's eyes. They quarrel bitterly and Odile rides to Aurenne. There her superior, Hesk, tells her the truth: a thousand gods were bound into one, and it has never been able to agree on a word. He had sent her to sign its part over to the Ledger. She refuses and is jailed under the Eye.

The Ledger offers Jess terms. Jess, worn down, performs without rest until she collapses, and the Widow uses the last of herself to keep her alive. Lucan explains that Jess is a precedent the Ledger has to own and end in public. The Ledger now holds 452 parts. For once Jess stops performing and tells Chat the plain truth, and nobody leaves. She sends the Ledger a one-word answer.

Then, with Mags and a guilty Quell, she goes through the house book and wakes the holders one by one: Drury the ferryman, two seamstresses, an elderly judge who knows the Cathedral's drains, and young Pip. She also quietly pays for the knee of Tam Rook, a seventeen-year-old she crippled in a pit fight because Chat voted for no mercy. The Gallery where Chat sits is fuller than it has ever been. The Widow's seat is kept for her, and the empty seat at the end of the bench now leans slightly toward the stage.
