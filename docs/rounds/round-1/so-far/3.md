Jess is a warlock without a single great patron. Instead she has hundreds of small ones, all watching her at once, and she calls them Chat. She talks to them constantly. Gristlemouth wants her to bite things, Mothlight rates her form, the Widow fusses, Brindle always greets her first, and Twelve-Pence keeps count of her money. Her power comes from their attention, paid for in tithes and won through polls, and it hits back when it fails. Waist-deep in Marrow Fen she lets Chat vote between punch and fire, burns a bog-wight for nine crowns, and still falls in the peat. From the reeds, a Vigilant named Odile watches her thank an empty fen.

Odile arrests her at the village inn, politely and by the book. In the lockup Jess explains Chat and tells her usual story: she was an actress who dried on stage in Saltreach and made up a line. Odile writes it down as evidence. On the road to Aurenne, chained wrist to wrist with Odile, Jess notices that Brindle has gone quiet. Twelve-Pence admits that several regulars have fallen silent this spring.

At the Sedge ferry they meet Lucan Thrale, whom Jess knows and fears. He tells her the Ledger is buying her piece by piece. "You said a line that wasn't in the script, Jess. Somebody had to pay for it." A ferryman, Abel Drury, pulls them from the river and looks at Jess as if he has dreamt her. A Notary reads out the Register of her pact. The Ledger holds 180 of a thousand parts and 340 lie dormant. The largest part, 248, belongs to a silent Witness who watches "through the eyes of another". When Jess names the pact night, at the Gilded Lamp in Saltreach in 612, Odile goes still. At fifteen, Odile ran away to see that very show and watched it end in fire. She tells no one. Instead she unchains Jess. In their gallery in the Beyond, the patrons turn away brokers, though Mothlight pockets a broker's card. Twelve-Pence confesses that he has known about the buying since winter.

Jess rebuilds Twelve-Pence's shrine at Pennyfold. Brother Cray arrives with an order to kill her where she stands, and Odile refuses to obey it. At Hask Bridge Mothlight sells his part, dozens of others flee after him, and Jess falls. The Widow catches her and spends most of herself doing it. Odile kills a Vigilant to hold the bridge. Odile then pays a second Notary with her own stipend. The dormant parts, it turns out, belong to the audience of that night, and whoever holds more than half can call the pact. The Ledger now holds 241. Odile realises she is one of those holders and stays silent.

At the burned Lamp, Jess finds a fresh bill in the hand of Quell, her old company master. Desperate for regard, she wins a pit fight and, because Chat voted "no mercy", breaks a seventeen-year-old's knee. Odile says nothing. In Lockwater, Quell confesses that he pledged "the best of my company", meaning Jess. He hands over the house book but lies about the promptbook. Odile works out that eleven holders have died without heirs, so their parts are falling to the Ledger. Jess and Odile reach the baker Mags Tolley ahead of Cray. Mags claims her part and becomes the first mortal to speak in Chat. Cray escapes with his list.

In the Ledger's counting-house, Lucan sees the Archvigilant's name and a list of mortals crossed out. He refuses to kill for the Ledger, and she takes a year of his life. He has hated Jess for seven years, and why is still unknown.
