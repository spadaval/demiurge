1. s01-live: Chat, We're Live
   Cold open in the drowned cellars under the Lowmarket. Tilly, live to three hundred and eighteen small gods, wades toward an old Sixth Canal shrine-box that Mother Sluice has nagged her into fetching, doing patter the whole way. The drift scrolls at the edge of her sight: Bellwether's AGAIN, Gnash heckling every slip, Old Hob correcting the date on a lintel, Thimble's quiet coin to darn her torn glove, Pip's tiny 'you used to tell me everything'. A drowner rises out of the mud. She's losing. Then gold from Greyhand, the patron who never speaks, and the whole of Chat roars through her, and she blows the drowner apart. In the box: a vellum leaf of old pact-law that makes Sluice go very quiet. Sluice: 'One day you'll do what you owe us.' Tilly climbs a ladder to a grate, still talking to Chat, and a Warden's lantern finds her face.
2. s02-straight: Let Me Get This Straight
   Casimir's eyes. Tannery Yard at night, a girl in a red-and-gold ribbon who keeps answering people who aren't there. He has been tracking unlicensed spirit-sign since the second hollowing. He questions her; she jokes; he extracts the shape of it: no single patron but three hundred and eighteen lesser ones, watching at once, throwing her power, and she calls them Chat. 'Wait. Let me get this straight.' He tells her about the hollowed ferryman and the lamplighter and watches her joke die. She tells him she fell into the Sump alone. Asked about his own patron, he says the Abbess of Hours has never spoken to him, and Tilly, who has never had a silent minute, looks at him with a pity he resents. Old Hob tells her where the Lamp Row ladder was moved from, a detail of his case no one outside the Wardens knows. While his watch strikes its nine bells she does the vanishing queen with herself, and is gone, leaving a playbill in his hand.
3. s03-kettle: Kettle Street
   Home at one in the morning to the Kettle Street Marionette Theater: dusty puppets, Rook drinking by the phantasmagoria lantern, Wick asleep on the prop-trunk because his mother's lodging is full; Rook sends him off to his crate in the arches. Rook reads the ledger leaf and goes grey and says nothing useful. Rook's rule about bleeding. Tilly tells Rook, for Chat's benefit, how she named them after the stonechats on the Foundling House wall. The empty cot in the loft that nobody mentions. Grey fingerless gloves under her pillow. She unties her ribbon, says 'Curtain', and the drift goes dark and kind. The scene ends on the dark and nothing else.
4. s04-provost: The Provost
   Morning at the Warden-House on Marrowgate Square. Crane, milk at the rims of her eyes, hears his report on the many-patroned girl and orders her found and hooded. A third victim was found this morning: a boy. Crane explains the many-mouthed suspicion and the Synod's terms: the Lamplight Jubilee will crown the bicentennial with a Final Tidying if the Concord assents and the Wardens show cause. She gives the Synod's account of Marrowgate as the reason she lives. Afterward Casimir climbs the dead Saint Aldo bell-tower, winds his nine-bell watch, and asks his patron, for the four-thousandth time, whether he did right. Silence.
5. s05-wick: Wick
   Tilly wakes late, heavy as if she hasn't slept, with canal mud on her boots she puts down to the delving. Bettany Fross comes up the stairs: Wick, in the arches, eyes white, smiling at nothing. Tilly sits with him and holds his hands, and he smiles at the wrong place. Chat goes wild with grief and anger; Gnash for once says nothing. Greyhand throws gold 'for vengeance', the first gold it has ever thrown without a fight. Tilly swears out loud, to all of them, that she'll find whoever did it. Then she goes looking for the Warden.
6. s06-tower: The Bell-Tower
   She finds Casimir alone in the Saint Aldo bell-tower and Bellwether, whose bell this was, goes silent for the first time in two years. She offers a deal: Chat's two-hundred-year memory of the city for his Warden's access, together, to find the hollower, and he keeps her off his report. He hates it and agrees: three victims, no leads. Mother Sluice calls a vote; Chat ship them noisily; Gnash votes no on principle. As they shake on it, a new patron takes the small oath: a lurker with a sigil of nine small bells. Tilly welcomes it on air and forgets about it. Casimir's watch strikes the hour.
7. s07-hospice: Saint Lenn's
   The long ward of the hollowed at the hospice of Saint Lenn: the ferryman, the lamplighter, Wick, fed with spoons by nuns. Old Hob, appalled, recognises the work: the many-mouthed draw, which can only be done through a window with many patrons. Casimir says that is exactly why the Wardens suspect small gods, and Chat take it as an accusation and the drift turns ugly. Tilly defends them to his face. Under the hospice smell of lye, all three victims smell of Sump silt.
8. s08-account: The Small Gods' Account
   The Kettle Street roof at dusk. Tilly relays, in voices, Mother Sluice's and Old Hob's account of the Tidying: shrines torn down, gods tipped into the quarry to starve, the starving then called dangerous. Of the Gallery: the Boxholders in velvet boxes and the small gods in the cheap seats. Of how starved gods go feral. Casimir answers with the Synod's account and Marrowgate. Neither convinces the other. He asks how she got out of the Sump, and gets the joke version, alone, low-budget, and notices she doesn't look at him while she says it.
9. s09-sump: The Sump
   They go down into the flooded quarry looking for a feral god. Tilly stands on the rubble shelf where she made the pact, among the broken idols, and talks too much, and does not look at one particular place on the shelf. Hungry Tom, the gallows-god, comes out of the water, all rope and hunger. Greyhand throws gold. Instead of blasting him she lets him drink a sip of sight through her eyes: the sunset over the quarry rim. He quiets enough to speak: not me; the velvet one walks in a sleeping girl's skin; it wears her down to the water at night. Tilly assumes some other girl. Casimir writes it down.
10. s10-five-hundred: Five Hundred and Forty
   Word of the girl who fed a feral god a sunset runs through the Gallery and strange spirits pour in from all over the Hush and beyond the city: the pact swells past five hundred. Tilly plays to them, bigger every time: walking the Brede weir-chain over white water, catching a crossbow bolt for a bet. Greyhand's gold comes every time. She notices its sigil, a grey glove, used to be a smudge, and lets herself wonder, privately, whether a fire-eater who always wore grey gloves could have become a god. Rook, watching her come home soaked and laughing, warns her: an audience grown on spectacle eats spectacle, and then it eats you.
11. s11-thread: A Thread of Silk
   Crane calls Casimir to her office and puts a red-and-gold silk thread on her blotter, taken from the nail of the crate where the boy was found. She asks if he has seen the many-patroned girl again. Then, unprompted, she tells him about Hester, sixteen, and the night Marrowgate burned, and why she will see every small god starved before another child dies. Casimir knows the silk. He says no. It is the first lie he has told her.
12. s12-roofs: Curtain, for Once
   An all-night stakeout over the Lowmarket roofs. Nothing happens, which nobody notices is the clue. Toward dawn, cold and tired, Casimir asks what it is like never to be alone. She asks what it is like always to be. For the first time Tilly says 'Curtain' not to sleep or to grieve but to talk, and the drift goes dark, and she asks him a real question and gives him a real answer, nearly: she almost tells him about the Sump, and doesn't. 'House up.' The drift floods back full of hearts and Gnash's retching.
13. s13-shares: The Law of Shares
   Old Hob, sober as a stair, reads the ledger leaf aloud: a window belongs to whoever has paid most into it, and a sleeping window is ridden by the greatest share among the eyes left open. Mother Sluice asks how much gold Greyhand has thrown. Tilly laughs it off and won't refuse it; gold is why she is alive. Sluice, stung, reminds her of the Sump: you promised us the city. Tilly says she was dying and would have promised the moon, and the drift goes very quiet. That night she says 'Curtain' and sleeps.
14. s14-silt: Silt
   Word at the Warden-House: a dockhand, Jory Tamsett, hollowed at the Weir Docks the night after the stakeout. Casimir takes the thread from evidence, borrows Tilly's ribbon on a pretext and matches the weave under a glass, and scrapes Sump silt from the treads of her boots while she sleeps in the theater stalls. He sits with the two things in his hands and does not wake her.
15. s15-queen: The Vanishing Queen
   Tilly visits Wick in the ward and does the vanishing queen for his blank white eyes, patter and all, because he would have wanted the patter. Thimble mends the torn elbow of his coat through her fingers, one quiet coin at a time. Gnash jeers at her for performing to a turnip. Tilly snaps and tells Gnash to leave; Chat take sides, Bellwether and Pip for her, a handful for Gnash. She leaves the queen of lamps on Wick's pillow.
16. s16-hood: The Hood
   Dawn. Wardens kick in the Kettle Street doors. Crane reads the charge: Nell Brisk, hollowed on the old canal steps tonight, said 'the conjurer girl' before her eyes went; a patrol saw a red ribbon. Rook goes for a Warden with a puppet-stand and is clubbed down. Casimir is in the doorway and does not move. The last thing Tilly sees is his face; then the hood comes down and three hundred voices are cut off mid-word.
17. s17-dark: In the Dark
   Four days under the hood in a Warden cell. Without her eyes Chat can see nothing, and she can feel them thinning. She does what she did in the Sump: she talks, describes, tells them stories, the cell by touch and the bread by taste, Rook's whole act from memory, because a story is a little like sight. Mother Sluice, faint, notices aloud that Greyhand is not thinning at all. On the third night Thimble mends the hem of Tilly's sleeve in the dark and goes out, and the brass thimble on its string goes cold against her breastbone.
18. s18-cheap-seats: The Gods in the Cheap Seats
   Feverish, Tilly slips backward through her own window and sees the Gallery. Tier on tier of tiny gods crammed into the high cheap seats, threadbare, leaning forward; far below, the velvet boxes of the Boxholders. Every box is lit but one, whose curtain is drawn. And among the small gods in the cheap seats, too large for its seat and keeping very still, a grey glove. She wakes not sure she has seen anything.
19. s19-cause: Shown Cause
   Crane, satisfied, tells Casimir the case is closed: no one has been hollowed since the girl was hooded. At the Jubilee she will be Veiled before the city beneath the Oculus, and through her severed window Vessary will draw and end every small god in the Hush; the Synod and the Concord have assented. Casimir asks why Vessary needs her alive and connected for that. Crane has no answer and does not want one. That night in the bell-tower he times the guard-change on the Abbess's watch.
20. s20-hood-off: Hood Off
   A hand on the back of her neck in the dark, Casimir's voice, the hood pulled off, and the drift crashes back in, starving, sobbing, four hundred and two where there were five hundred and forty. She feels the gaps like missing teeth. A Warden on the stair. Chat have nothing to throw; Greyhand throws gold, and she blows the cell-block door off its hinges. They run through the Lowmarket arches into the night, both of them outlaws now.
21. s21-canal-bed: Mother Sluice's Bed
   They hide in the brick vaults under the buried Sixth Canal, which is Mother Sluice's old bed; she is quiet the whole first day. Bettany brings Rook, bruised and furious and alive. Tilly mourns Thimble with a joke that doesn't land, and then without one. Casimir tells her what Crane said about the Jubilee. Rook asks Casimir why he came back for her; he doesn't know how to answer.
22. s22-glove: The Glove Speaks
   Mother Sluice, grave, tells Tilly what the leaf means for her: Greyhand now holds nearly half her pact. Tilly asks Greyhand, on air, to stop throwing gold. For the first time Greyhand speaks in the drift, softly: it was the god of the Glovers' Hall, burned in the Tidying; it only wants her safe; a pact can't be broken from one side. Tilly, who had let herself hope it was Dov, is disappointed, and ashamed of it, and almost likes the thing. Casimir watches her face and decides to stay awake tonight.
23. s23-night-watch: The Night Watch
   Casimir sits awake while Tilly sleeps behind the Curtain. In the small hours she gets up. Her eyes are open and milky. She walks, quick and silent, out of the vault and along the canal-cut to Cobb's Bridge, where a man sleeps in rags, kneels, and takes his face in her hands. Casimir grabs her and her body hits him with the Pit's Roar, a voice that isn't hers saying his name. He drags her into the black water of the cut. She comes up gasping and awake, standing over a man with one white eye, her own thumbs on his lids.
24. s24-i-did-this: I Did This
   Morning. Tilly counts back: the mud on her boots, the nights she said Curtain, the thread. Wick smiled because he saw her. She tells Chat with no Curtain. The drift erupts: Gnash's 'we said', others shouting it was one of them, Mother Sluice demanding every patron stand and declare. Greyhand says only, 'I can see I'm not welcome,' and goes silent. Patrons leave by the score; the pact falls to two hundred and sixty-one. Casimir sits by her and says nothing useful, which is the right thing. That night, for the first time since the Sump, she sleeps without the Curtain, and Mother Sluice sets the ones who stayed to watch her in shifts.
25. s25-needle: The Needle
   Alone in the old lime-kiln, Tilly holds a lime-burner's needle to her eye to shut the window for good so she can never be ridden again. The drift begs. Then the smallest voice, Pip, says a whole sentence for the first time: you said you'd never close your eyes on me again. And she remembers the crack behind her cot in the Foundling House, and the friend she told everything, and forgot. She puts the needle down.
26. s26-velvet: The Velvet Eye
   They put it together on the vault floor with chalk. Greyhand didn't thin in the hood, so it has other windows. Hungry Tom's 'velvet one'. The empty box with its curtain drawn. Old Hob: the Glovers' Hall had no small god, only a licensed Boxholder altar, and the grey smudge became a glove the morning after its first Curtain night. Casimir: the Synod calls Vessary the Velvet Eye. Greyhand is Vessary. The hollowings were a harvest and a frame, and the Final Tidying will go through Tilly's window into the whole Hush.
27. s27-marrowgate: Marrowgate
   Rook, sober for the first time in years, tells them what he saw from the roofs of Marrowgate Lane at twenty: the Lamb was real and terrible, three people walked into the well; and then Vessary's warlocks sealed both ends and lit it, and twenty-eight burned. He testified and it was sealed. He kept Tilly afraid of the Wardens rather than angry at them because angry gets you burned. Casimir, who has believed the Synod's account all his life, goes out and stands in the rain.
28. s28-lens: A Lens That Gathers
   Rook explains the Oculus as only a lantern-showman could: a lens that gathers light onto the altar can throw it outward, and with the phantasmagoria lantern's mirrors and enough power, the sight of a warlock at the focus could be thrown on every wall in Sallowcross. If the city sees the small gods, its attention will feed them, and no Final Tidying can starve what a whole city is looking at. Tilly realises this is how she keeps the promise she has been dodging. Casimir will go to Crane. And Chat must grow, honestly, enough to outweigh Greyhand.
29. s29-true-story: The True Story of the Sump
   Tilly goes live to the whole Hush, no stunts, and tells the Sump as it was: the raid, the fall, Dov's back, the second evening, a day talking to a dead boy, the idols, the promise, climbing out and leaving him on the shelf. She holds his grey gloves. No trick in it, and she lets them see her bleeding. Small gods from every bridge and well of the city who had never heard of her come to listen and take the oath; the ones who left come back; the pact passes fourteen hundred. Greyhand's share drops. Gnash throws a coin and doesn't say why.
30. s30-proof: Proof
   Casimir walks into Crane's house at midnight with the one-eyed man from Cobb's Bridge, Rook's sealed Marrowgate statement taken from the archive with his old keys, and the thread. He tells her her patron has been riding a sleeping girl through the city. Crane arrests him in her own parlour and then, with him in irons in her chair, reads every page.
31. s31-reliquary: The Reliquary
   Crane goes down beneath the Oculus at three in the morning with the Provost's keys. The Lidless do not stop her; they think she is Vessary's. Shelves of sealed jars, each with a name in a clerk's hand she knows: Gorrie, Pellin, Amberley. She asks her patron. For the first time in thirty-four years Vessary answers her in words: the jars are what the small-god warlock stole, recovered. She can see that it's a lie. She thinks of Hester in Marrowgate Lane and the fire at both ends.
32. s32-lamplight: Lamplight
   The Jubilee: lanterns on every bridge, the whole city pressing uphill toward the Oculus. Tilly rides inside a giant pageant puppet worked by the Kettle Street Players, Rook at the lantern somewhere above. Chat, fourteen hundred strong, trying to be quiet. Inside the cathedral the Lidless, who see with Vessary's eyes and so knew she was coming, open the puppet like a box and bind her at the focus beneath the Eye.
33. s33-tidying: The Final Tidying
   The Lidless cut her so she will need power to live and break the Reliquary jars over the focus. Stolen sight floods through Tilly's window toward the Hush and the law counts every drop as Greyhand's; its share climbs to forty-nine in the hundred. It speaks to her alone, kind as ever: give it the window and it will spare Pip and the ushers. She says no. Chat throw everything into the hat to hold it under half. Mother Sluice gives all she is and goes out mid-sentence. Gnash, cursing, gives it all: nobody heckles her but me. Bellwether rings. Pip gives. It is still not enough: Greyhand rises level.
34. s34-nine-bells: Nine Bells
   Casimir comes in under the dome at Crane's side with the Wardens she has turned out. Crane declares the rite unlicensed under the charter and orders the Lidless taken; Vessary takes the rest of her sight and she goes on giving orders blind. Tilly, at the focus, bleeding, looks straight at him and says in a voice that is hers and not hers: twelve years I looked out through you and never once saw you; these forty days I have looked at nothing else. His watch strikes nine. Nine Bells throws an archpatron's gold and the balance breaks; the pact casts Greyhand out.
35. s35-city-sees: The City Sees
   Rook unshutters the lantern. Tilly gives the city her eyes: on every wall and sail and wet cobble in Sallowcross, people see what Chat sees, and then see Chat, the small gods crowded in the cheap seats of the Gallery, and the velvet box with Vessary shrinking in it. The city looks back. Thousands of forgotten small gods are seen by mortal eyes for the first time in two hundred years, Hungry Tom among them, and fed. Vessary, seen as it is, shrinks into its box. Beneath the floor the Reliquary's seals fail and stolen sight runs home.
36. s36-queen-of-lamps: The Queen of Lamps
   Morning in Saint Lenn's. Wick opens his eyes, and the first thing he sees is the queen of lamps on his pillow. The aftermath in pieces: the Synod in uproar, Tull's resignation, the Concord suspended, Crane blind and giving evidence, the Wardens' charter reopened. Chat without Mother Sluice. Casimir tells Tilly that the Abbess spoke to him once, directly, one sentence, and it didn't burn. He doesn't say what it was.
37. s37-sixth-canal: The Sixth Canal
   Spring. The city clears the rubble from the Sixth Canal and lets the water in, and crowds come to watch it fill. Washerwomen kneel on the steps. Out of all that attention a small new trickle of Mother Sluice comes back, not remembering much, and scolds Tilly anyway. Tilly sets Thimble's cold brass thimble in a niche on the steps. The pact is two hundred and ninety-one, the ones who stayed because they wanted to. She says 'Curtain' because she wants a moment, and kisses Casimir, and they keep it. 'House up. Alright, Chat.'
