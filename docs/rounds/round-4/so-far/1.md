Tilly is a warlock with no single patron. Instead, three hundred and eighteen small gods watch her at once and lend her power, and she calls them Chat. Their comments scroll at the edge of her sight. Bellwether shouts AGAIN, Gnash heckles, Old Hob corrects her history, and tiny Pip says sadly, "You used to tell me everything." Mother Sluice leads them. Greyhand never speaks but sometimes throws gold, and then the whole of Chat pours through her.

Sluice sent Tilly into the drowned cellars to fetch an old shrine-box. Greyhand's gold saved her from a drowner, and the box held a leaf of old pact-law that made Sluice warn her, "One day you'll do what you owe us." Climbing out, Tilly was caught by Casimir, a Warden hunting whoever is "hollowing" people, leaving them alive but empty-eyed. His own patron has never spoken to him. Tilly told him she fell into the Sump, the flooded quarry, alone, then vanished.

She lives at the Kettle Street Marionette Theater with Rook, who went grey reading the pact-law but said nothing useful. A cot in the loft stays empty and nobody mentions it, and she keeps grey fingerless gloves under her pillow.

Provost Crane ordered Tilly found and hooded. Crane suspects a power with many patrons is behind the hollowings. If the Wardens can show cause, the Synod plans a "Final Tidying" of the small gods. The next morning Tilly woke exhausted, with canal mud on her boots she put down to the cellars, and learned that the boy Wick, who often slept at the theater, had been hollowed. Greyhand threw gold "for vengeance," and Tilly swore to find whoever did it.

In the Saint Aldo bell-tower she offered Casimir a deal: Chat's memory of the city for his Warden's access, and he keeps her out of his report. Chat voted yes. As they shook hands, a silent new watcher joined, its sigil nine small bells, the number Casimir's watch strikes.

At Saint Lenn's hospice, Old Hob recognised the hollowing as a "many-mouthed draw," possible only through someone with many patrons. All three victims smelled of Sump silt. Tilly and Casimir traded the small gods' account of the old Tidying and the Synod's account, and neither convinced the other. In the Sump, the feral god Hungry Tom, calmed by a sunset Tilly let him see through her eyes, said that the velvet one walks in a sleeping girl's skin and wears her down to the water at night. Tilly assumed he meant another girl. Casimir wrote it down.

Word spread, and Chat swelled past five hundred. Tilly performs ever bigger stunts, and Greyhand's gold comes every time. Its sigil has sharpened into a grey glove, and she privately wonders whether a fire-eater who always wore grey gloves could have become a god. Rook warns her that an audience grown on spectacle eats spectacle, and then eats you.
