Bry is a warlock with no single patron. Instead she has hundreds of lesser ones, all watching her at once, and she calls them Chat. Their messages scroll up past her left eye. Gorehound spams FIGHT IT. The Left Hand tuts. The Nine Wet Gentlemen argue about the tide. Lantern gives directions that are right about half the time, and Thimble keeps the rude ones in line. Chat votes on what she should do, sends sparks of every colour when she does well, and keeps a running count of who's watching. She wears a mirror shard on a cord. We first meet her knee-deep in sludge under Vossick's tannery, hunting ghouls. Because of the fumes, Chat votes for frost over fire, and she freezes the nest with 362 watching. Afterwards a pale silver voice called Mothlight asks if she's eaten today. She hasn't.

Dov, who runs the Leaning Gull, pays her in stew and grumbling. She sings there, taking requests from Chat. A sour performer named Lark Sabine asks how she does it, and Bry laughs the question off. At night she writes the day's new names in her guestbook, with Chat helping her spell them, and burns her toast. She sleeps with the window open, as she always does, while Chat murmurs her to sleep. Mothlight says "goodnight, little k—", but the scroll moves on before Bry notices.

Then a man named Corvin walks into the Gull with a writ. Dov tries to throw him out. Bry, curious, explains herself, and Corvin can hardly believe it: hundreds of patrons, and she calls them Chat? Chat names him Crowboy. The moment he comes in, Mothlight goes silent. The writ carries a hawthorn seal, and Bry goes cold without knowing why. She runs. In Ropewalk Alley, Chat votes FIGHT. Corvin is far better than she is, fighting with thorns and shadow, and he takes care not to hurt her. The fight draws watchers, her power spikes past four hundred, and she knocks him into the river.

She hides for two days with Chat as lookout. At dawn, Ferro's Pactwardens raid the Gull. From a rooftop, Bry watches Dov marched off to the customs house and the licence torn off the door, while Ferro gives the street a speech about the ropewalk fire. Her count dips. She can't perform, and when Chat turns careful and kind, she can't stand the quiet in her own head.

Corvin finds her on the chandler's roof above the garret where her mother left her. He's wet and dignified, but not angry. The Wardens, he tells her, are bound by only one ruling, the Concordat's. If she pleads at the Long Night Court and wins a licence, Dov goes free. She takes the writ, then takes out her mother's note. It has the same green wax and the same hawthorn. She agrees to go, but doesn't tell him why. Chat: ROAD TRIP.

They head north out of Lessering, past the propped-up bridge Chat keeps bringing up. Along the way they trade rules. Hers are the Floor, sparks, votes and the guestbook. His are that the Queen took his name, and that he carries six thorn beads, four of them white. He hears his patron twice a year, while she has never had a silent minute. When she asks for his name for her book, he says he has none to give.

At Hob's Crossing the flooded Sallow is tearing the toll bridge apart, and its keeper, Hal, is still on it trying to save the toll-box. Bry holds the arch while Chat votes HOLD, but it isn't enough. Then a faint grey-brown voice she has never seen speak tells her exactly how to set the keystone. It signs itself Quillon. Hal hears her say the name.

In his toll-house, beside an empty shrine whose hinges he oils every morning, Bry passes on Quillon's words exactly. Hal tests them and breaks down. Quillon tells him: I never left; I got too small to be heard. Hal explains an old name-witness law from his codex, which interests Corvin. In the morning he locks up and joins them, saying the Court will need someone who reads old law.

Then come six days of rain in which nothing happens, and for the first time in a year her count falls, to about three hundred. Bry tries stupider and stupider stunts to win them back, and Corvin watches her with something like pity. In Crake's market, Mr. Tallow, whose candle face drips as he smiles, offers her sponsorship from a principal representing "many small parties" and holds out a brass coin stamped with scales. Corvin warns her never to take a gift from the Gallery she hasn't earned, and Thimble doesn't like the man. She takes the coin anyway.
