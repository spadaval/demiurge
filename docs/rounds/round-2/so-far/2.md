Bry is a warlock with no single patron. Instead she has hundreds of small ones, all watching her at once, and she calls them Chat. Their words run up the inside of her left eye in a scrolling stream: Gorehound shouting FIGHT IT, the Left Hand tutting, the Nine Wet Gentlemen arguing about the tide, Lantern giving directions that are right about half the time. Thimble hushes the crude ones. Mothlight, pale silver, asks whether she has eaten today. Chat votes on what she should do, and its approval falls on her as sparks of every colour. The more of them watching, the stronger she is. She keeps a mirror shard on a cord, and every new name goes in her guestbook.

In the sludge under Vossick's tannery, Chat votes frost over fire because of the fumes, and Bry freezes a ghoul nest. At the Leaning Gull, Dov pays her in stew and grumbling, and she sings whatever Chat requests. Lark Sabine, sour in the corner, asks how she does it, and Bry laughs the question off. That night she writes the day's names in her book, burns her toast and falls asleep with the window open while Chat murmurs her to sleep. Mothlight says "goodnight, little k—", and the scroll moves on before Bry notices.

Then Corvin arrives with a writ. He can hardly believe how she works. Chat names him Crowboy, and Mothlight goes silent the moment he walks in. The writ carries a hawthorn seal that leaves Bry cold for reasons she can't name, so she runs. In Ropewalk Alley Chat votes FIGHT. Corvin is far better than her, all thorns and shadow, and careful not to hurt her. But the fight pulls in more watchers, her count climbs past four hundred, and she knocks him into the river.

Two days later Ferro's Pactwardens raid the Gull at dawn. Bry watches from a roof as Dov is marched to the customs house and the licence is torn off the door, while Ferro gives the street a speech about the ropewalk fire. Her count dips, and she can't bear how quiet her head goes when Chat turns careful and kind.

Corvin finds her on the chandler's roof above the garret where her mother left her. He is wet but not angry. If she pleads at the Long Night Court and wins a licence, he tells her, Dov goes free. Her mother's note is sealed with the same green wax and the same hawthorn. She agrees, and she doesn't tell him why.

On the north road the two trade rules. His Queen took his name. He wears six thorn beads, four of them white, and hears his patron twice a year. Bry has never had a silent minute. Neither can understand how the other bears it.

At Hob's Crossing the flooded Sallow is tearing apart the toll bridge, with its keeper, Hal, still on it. A faint grey-brown voice she has never seen speak tells Bry where to set the keystone and how to turn it, and signs itself Quillon. Hal, it turns out, is Quillon's priest. Quillon passes Hal tests only he could answer and tells him, "I never left; I got too small to be heard." Hal explains an old law about witnessing names, which catches Corvin's interest, and in the morning he joins them.

Six days of rain follow, and Bry's count drops to about three hundred. She tries ever more reckless stunts to win watchers back. In Crake's market Mr. Tallow, whose candle face drips when he smiles, offers her a brass coin stamped with scales. Corvin warns her against it, and Thimble doesn't like the man, but she takes it anyway. By the next morning 915 are watching, and the newcomers all give their name as "just a friend." They never disagree with each other. With them Bry lifts a broken mill-wheel out alone and lights every lamp in Crake at once. Mothlight asks if she's sure.

At Fennick's Ford, Lark is performing with a window of her own, filled with the voices Thimble used to hush. She is frightened and desperate for her father's medicine. Glowing brass-bright, Bry tells her to give them what they want. Lark's chat wants the Drowned Mill. That evening Bry follows Corvin and watches him spend an hour looking through a window at his brother Tobin without going in. She keeps it from Chat, and Chat sees it anyway.
