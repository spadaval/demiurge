1. s01-tannery: Under the Tannery
   Cold open, Bry's POV. Knee-deep in tannery sludge under Vossick's, Bry narrates a ghoul hunt to Chat, the scroll running up her left eye: Gorehound spamming FIGHT IT, the Left Hand tutting, the Nine Wet Gentlemen arguing about the tide, Lantern saying go left (right once, wrong once). Thimble hushes a lewd imp. Chat votes frost over fire because of the fumes; she freezes the nest; sparks of every colour. Establishes the Floor, the votes, the sparks, the mirror shard on its cord. Mothlight, pale silver, asks whether she has eaten today. She hasn't. 362 watching.
2. s02-gull: The Leaning Gull
   Dov pays her in stew and grumbling; she performs at the Gull, singing with Chat requesting songs. Lark Sabine in the corner, sour, asks how she does it; Bry laughs it off. At night in her room she writes the day's new names in the guestbook (Chat helps spell them) and burns toast. She falls asleep with the window open as always; Chat murmurs her to sleep. Mothlight says 'goodnight, little k—' and the scroll moves on before she notices.
3. s03-straight: Let Me Get This Straight
   Corvin walks into the Gull with the writ. Dov tries to throw him out; Bry, curious, explains herself with Chat heckling. Corvin says the premise's line: hundreds of lesser patrons, all watching, and she calls them Chat? Chat names him Crowboy. Mothlight goes silent the moment he enters. He serves the writ; she reads the hawthorn seal and feels cold without knowing why, then runs. In Ropewalk Alley Chat votes FIGHT; he is far better, thorns and shadow, and careful not to hurt her; the fight pulls watchers in, her power spikes past four hundred, and she knocks him into the river.
4. s04-raid: The Raid
   Two days hiding with Chat as lookout. Ferro's Pactwardens raid the Gull at dawn; Bry watches from a roof as Dov is marched to the customs house and the licence torn off the door. Ferro's speech to the street about the ropewalk fire. Bry's count dips; she can't perform; she can't stand the quiet in her own head when Chat goes careful and kind.
5. s05-rooftop: The Hawthorn Seal
   Night on the chandler's roof over the garret where her mother left her. Corvin finds her (shadow-step). He is wet, dignified and not angry. He tells her the only ruling that binds the Wardens is the Concordat's: plead at the Long Night Court, win a licence, and Dov is free. She takes the writ, then takes out her mother's note: the same green wax, the same hawthorn. She agrees. She doesn't tell him why. Chat: ROAD TRIP.
6. s06-road: The North Road
   Leaving Lessering at dawn past the propped-up span of the Lessering Bridge, which Chat won't stop bringing up. Banter: she explains the rules (Floor, sparks, votes, the guestbook); he explains his (the Queen took his name, six thorn beads, four white). He hears his patron twice a year; she has never had a silent minute. Neither understands how the other bears it. She asks his name for the book; he says he doesn't have one to give.
7. s07-hobs: Hob's Crossing
   The Sallow in flood; the toll bridge at Hob's Crossing coming apart with its keeper, Hal, on it trying to save the toll-box. Bry holds the arch with Chat voting HOLD; it isn't enough; then a voice in the scroll she has never seen speak, faint and grey-brown, tells her exactly where the keystone must sit and how to turn it. She sets it. The voice signs itself Quillon. Hal, on his knees on the wet stone, hears her say the name.
8. s08-tollhouse: Too Small to Be Heard
   In Hal's toll-house, by the empty shrine whose hinges he oils every morning, Bry relays Quillon word for word. Hal tests the voice (the hinge he always oils wrong, the sermon he never finished) and breaks. Quillon: I never left; I got too small to be heard. Hal explains, to Corvin's interest, the old name-witness law from his codex while arguing with his god about the Compendium. In the morning Hal locks up and comes, saying the Court will need someone who reads old law.
9. s09-dull: Six Days of Rain
   Nothing happens for six days and the count falls for the first time in a year, to about three hundred. Bry does stupider stunts for attention (juggles fire in the rain, picks a fight with a bull); Corvin watches her with something like pity, which is worse. In Crake's market, Mr. Tallow, his candle-face dripping as he smiles, offers sponsorship from 'a principal who represents many small parties who'd love to watch' and a brass coin stamped with scales. Corvin: never take a gift from the Gallery you haven't earned. Thimble doesn't like him. She takes it.
10. s10-surge: Just a Friend
   She wakes to 915 watchers. Hundreds of new voices, all lovely, all called 'just a friend', all tributing brass. She asks their names for the guestbook and they laugh: just a friend! She does things she never could: lifts a mill-wheel, lights a whole town's lamps. Chat's old regulars are drowned out; Cinder goes quiet; Mothlight asks if she's sure. Corvin says nothing, which she takes as jealousy.
11. s11-fennick: Lark at Fennick's Ford
   Lark Sabine is performing in the square at Fennick's Ford with a pawned mirror and a window of her own: her chat is the things Thimble hushed off Bry's, Gorehound among them. Lark is thin and frightened and furious; her father's medicine is due. She asks how Bry keeps hers. Bry, glowing and brass-bright, tells her: give them what they want. Lark's chat wants the Drowned Mill. That evening Corvin slips off; Lantern leads Bry after him to the new mill, where he watches his brother Tobin through a window for an hour and doesn't go in. She doesn't tease him. It is the first thing about him she doesn't tell Chat, and Chat sees it anyway.
12. s12-mill: The Drowned Mill
   Night. Lark goes into the Drowned Mill to fight the thing in the millpond with her chat screaming her on. It goes wrong. Bry and Corvin go in after her. Bry's chat-vote, brass-heavy, comes back SHOW IT (a great flashy burning) rather than PULL HER OUT; Bry hesitates one vote too long; Corvin dives; Lark drowns in the weed. Gorehound in both chats: BEST NIGHT. Bry's count reaches 1,460.
13. s13-millrace: When Did You Last Close It?
   Dawn by the mill-race, Lark's body under Corvin's coat. Corvin asks when she last closed the window. Never. She tells him the garret: the note, the waiting, the silence in which she counted the drips, and the solstice alley at seventeen when she sang into the shard and said 'Thimble?' and Thimble came; then everyone else came, uninvited, and she never shut the door again. Chat is quiet and decent for once. He tells her about Tobin and the hill-fever, and that silence is what he has instead of a family. She asks if they can be quiet together; they can't, she's never alone.
14. s14-thimble: Thimble's Suspicion
   Thimble, on her knee as a flame in the tinderbox lid, says privately (in the scroll, hushed so no one else sees) that the friends arrived together, never disagree, never give names, breathe their tribute at the same instant. One thing in hundreds of faces. Bry, who has never been so strong, accuses Thimble of jealousy at being one voice among thousands. Thimble goes into the tinderbox. That night, Tallow's candle-light comes through the window while she sleeps, following the token; Thimble's last message is half a word. In the morning the tinderbox is cold, and the scroll is a sewer.
15. s15-marsh: The Sallow Marsh
   Unmoderated Chat, howling. Ferro and two licensed warlocks ambush them in the reeds. The vote comes back BURN in a brass bloc; Bry remembers the ropewalk speech and refuses the vote for the first time, casting frost against the tribute's will; the backlash knocks her flat. Grandmother Cinder, who never speaks, pours in grey sparks: 'not like that, child. like this.' The frost holds. Ferro, saved from her own warlock's fire, lets them go: she wants to hear what the Court says.
16. s16-song: The Mirror-Song
   Corvin's POV. An inn at Harrowgate. Bry sings for Chat to win back the regulars, a mirror-song in an old Lessering tune, and Corvin knows it: he heard it through the silver wire of the Queen's songbird cage for eleven years. Flashback: the garret in 1300, the woman with the hand mirror, 'don't tell the child', a twelve-year-old asleep behind a curtain. He sees the shard on Bry's cord. He says nothing. At dawn the Queen speaks to him from a frozen puddle, returns a fifth letter as earnest, and asks: does the girl sing? No, Majesty.
17. s17-chantry: The Lion's Share
   The ruined chantry of Quillon at Sallowhead. Hal, with Quillon nagging, reads the Compendium and finds the Lion's Share. Bry counts colours for the first time: almost half her sparks are brass. If one power is behind them and passes half before the presiding Seat at the Court, it holds her whole. She looks at Corvin, who works for a Seat, and wonders.
18. s18-crowman: The Crow-Man
   Corvin off scouting. Mothlight, who has always gone quiet around him, writes: the crow-man took the singer of Lessering into the Hill. I saw. Bry knows one singer of Lessering who vanished. When Corvin comes back she asks him plainly. He tells her: he took her mother to the Hill, she came willingly so the Queen wouldn't take Bry's voice, she asked him never to say. The vote comes back KILL HIM, brass and Gorehound; Bry's fire and thorn nearly do it; Hal stands in the way with his arms out. Next morning she sends Corvin back to his Queen.
19. s19-contract: Small Print
   Bry and Hal alone, Bry streaming harder and more recklessly than ever, sleeping badly. Tallow returns to a roadside fire with a contract: formal sponsorship, and his principal will buy her mother out of the Hill. She has the pen in her hand. Quillon, god of tolls, who has read ten thousand toll-charters, makes Hal read the small print aloud: a party holding the greater part holds the whole. Hal sees the scales on the seal and finds them in the codex: the Brass Marquis. Tallow smiles, drips, and says, 'You'll sign at the Court anyway, friend. That's the beauty of it.'
20. s20-hill-corvin: The Empty Cage
   Corvin's POV. Under the Hill, a week ahead of the Court. Halls of root and frost. The silver songbird cage is empty. The Queen tells him, fondly, that the singer thinned and flew eighteen months ago, and that she will put the girl in it: a thousand voices in one throat. She knew whose daughter Bry was all along; she sent him on purpose. He asks for nothing. Walking out, he decides to betray his patron.
21. s21-greyford: The Tollhouse at Grey Ford
   Bry and Hal break into the Marquis's counting-house at Grey Ford, ledgers and brass scales, to free Thimble. Tallow fights with a seal-knife. Mid-spell every mask withholds its tribute at once: her power halves, her fire gutters; Tallow laughs. Hal, bleeding, prays aloud for the first time in eight years, and Quillon, god of narrow crossings, spends all he has opening the neck of the brass bottle. Thimble bursts out as a flame and sets the ledgers alight. They run. Quillon's voice in the scroll is fainter than ever.
22. s22-hut: Thimble's Report
   A shepherd's hut in the snow; Hal feverish from the seal-knife. Thimble, singed, reports what it heard in the bottle: the Marquis will claim at midnight on the solstice before the Unblinking, when he'll be well past half; he means to own crowds. Hushing does nothing; the friends have no names to bar. Hal, delirious, asks what becomes of a pact if the window is closed. Bry won't answer. She sits up all night with the window open, listening to Chat, terrified of the alternative.
23. s23-return: Something Pale With Wings
   Corvin finds the hut. Bry nearly blasts him. He tells her what he learned: her mother escaped the Hill a year and a half ago, thinned into something pale with wings, and the Queen knew who Bry was and wants her caged. Bry thinks of silver sparks, of 'have you eaten', of a moth that goes quiet when a crow comes in. He doesn't ask forgiveness. She doesn't give it. She lets him stay.
24. s24-kettle: Hello, Little Kettle
   Bry asks Mothlight, out loud, in front of all of Chat. Long pause in the scroll. Then: hello, little kettle. Hesper remembers songs and burnt toast and a curtain and not much else; she types slowly, misremembers, apologises. She tells Bry she was never left. Be loud, she said, and you were. Chat, for once, says nothing, then floods with hearts, and Bry hushes the brass ones herself. Hesper says she knows the crow-man's true name from the Hill, and that it is not hers to give. Bry writes in the guestbook: Mothlight (Mum).
25. s25-eve: The Last Night Out
   The night before they go under. Hal, mending, can't sleep; Bry reads him the guestbook to pass the time, names and notes (Lantern: always says go left; the Wet Gentlemen: do not mention the cook), and Chat chimes in as each is read. The brass ones aren't in it. Corvin listens from the door. She asks his name for the book again; he says the Queen holds it, and that he'd give it to her if he could. Thimble, from the tinderbox: mind the Hill's rules.
26. s26-court: The Long Night Court
   Under the Hill: eat nothing, give no name, thank no one. The Court in a hall of frozen roots: the Unblinking's vast eye, the Hollow Queen in white, the Brass Marquis in person, beautiful and courteous. The Queen names the crowd-pact an abomination and asks that it be unmade, and adds, smiling, that the girl's mother sang better. The Marquis, to Bry's horror, argues eloquently that the pact is lawful. Bry pleads: for Dov, for the small powers who have nowhere else to look. The Unblinking will rule at midnight.
27. s27-claim: Midnight
   At midnight the Marquis rises and invokes the Lion's Share. The Unblinking counts: 2,104 patrons, 1,163 his. Brass rises in her like water; the scroll is all 'just a friend'; she feels her own hands begin to be someone else's. The Queen, robbed, is white with fury. Hal shouts law; it's correct law, and it's against them.
28. s28-close: Close It
   Bry takes the mirror shard off its cord and turns it face down on the frozen floor. The window closes. Silence for the first time in seven years: no scroll, no murmur, the inside of her own head. The pact empties; the Marquis's claim has nothing to hold, and he is, briefly, amused. She has no power at all. The Queen steps down for her songbird. Corvin steps between and claims custody under the writ until the Court has ruled; the Queen takes back every letter of his name and the beads go black at his throat; he doesn't move.
29. s29-litany: The Litany
   Bry remembers the alley: she said 'Thimble?' and Thimble came. She opens the guestbook and reopens the window not to all comers but by calling, each name aloud. Thimble, first, a flame in the tinderbox. Grandmother Cinder. Lantern-in-the-Fen (go left). The Nine Wet Gentlemen, all nine. Saint Ambry's Left Hand. Old Quillon, almost nothing, who grows as six hundred voices hear his name. Gorehound, who comes, and is quiet. Mothlight. Six hundred and fourteen names, three hours, her voice going. The friends answer only to 'just a friend', which nobody is calling, and cannot come in. The count climbs, every one of them true.
30. s30-anselm: Anselm
   The last page. Corvin is nameless, beadless, still standing. In the scroll, Hesper writes a word and says: now it's his to want, and yours to say. Bry says Anselm before six hundred and fourteen witnesses. A name freely given by someone who loves him can never be owned again. The beads bloom white all at once. Anselm laughs, which nobody in the Hill has heard him do.
31. s31-ruling: The Ruling
   The Unblinking rules by the letter: the Marquis holds no part of a pact whose every patron is named, since the window he claimed was closed and this is a new pact; a crowd-pact whose patrons are named is lawful; the Queen has no claim on a warlock without a pact nor a man whose name is witnessed. Then the Unblinking, which finds very little interesting, says: interesting. The Concordat sits in shocked silence: lesser powers can now pool lawfully. The Queen leaves without a word. The Marquis bows to Bry and says he is very patient. They walk out into the first morning after the longest night.
32. s32-crossing: The Shrine at Hob's Crossing
   January. Hal rebuilds Quillon's shrine at the toll bridge and oils the hinge correctly. With one priest and six hundred watchers who know his name, Quillon is big enough to speak to Hal himself. Hal stays.
33. s33-lessering: Home
   Lessering. Ferro reads the ruling and opens Dov's cell herself; Bry gets the first licence ever issued to a crowd-warlock. The Gull reopens. She visits Lark's grave and begins a register: anyone who wants to open a window must learn to do it by name. Mothlight, named and silver, watches her mother's old garret through her daughter's eyes.
34. s34-goodnight: Goodnight, Chat
   Mid-January. Anselm has been to Fennick's Ford and told Tobin, using his own name. An evening at the Gull. Anselm asks nothing. Bry tells Chat she's going to close the window for the night. The scroll: gn bry. gn. go left. not like that, child. gn little kettle. She turns the shard face down. The quiet is not the garret's quiet. Close on the two of them, unwatched.
