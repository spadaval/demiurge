The Drowned Mill stood a mile below Fennick's Ford, where the Sallow had changed its mind forty years ago and walked off east, leaving the old mill sitting in its own black pond like a man up to his chest in a bath gone cold. The roof was gone. The wheel was still there, half sunk, furred with weed, and the millpond around it was so thick with weed that on a moonlit night it looked like a field you could walk across. And the count climbed. She felt it go up through her jaw in a long, rising whine: one thousand and fifty, one thousand two hundred, one thousand three hundred, as across the Gallery word went from window to window that a girl had set a lake on fire.

The fire did nothing at all to what was underneath.

She understood it as she watched. Fire ran over the top of water; that was what fire did. It was why the regulars had said *pull*. The weed burned and curled and blackened on the surface, and below it the pond was as dark and cold as ever, and somewhere in the dark the Grindle had Lark Sabine by the ankles and was taking her down to where the old millstones lay.

Then the burning weed broke apart in the middle of the pond, and Corvin came up through it.
