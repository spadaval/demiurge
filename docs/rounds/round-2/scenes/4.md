In the morning she asked Corvin to go and get wood.

There was wood. There was a stack of it by the door that would see them to the solstice. He looked at it, and then at her, and then he put on his coat, which was still damp, and went out into the snow without a word, and she heard his steps go away round the side of the hut and keep going until she couldn't hear them any more.

Hal was sitting up on his pallet with a bowl of broth he wasn't drinking. He had his spectacles on. He didn't pretend not to watch her.

"Do you want me to go too?" he said.

"No." She sat down on the floor by the fire, cross-legged, the way she used to sit on the Gull's stage when she was working out a new song. "No. Stay. Somebody should be here who isn't in my head."

The tinderbox was on the hearthstone beside her. Thimble's flame stood up in it, very still.

*Thimble: bry?*

"It's all right." It wasn't. Her heart was going the slow hard way again. She put her hands flat on her knees to stop them, and looked at the scroll, and the scroll was the scroll: seventeen hundred and some, murmuring *morning* and *crowboy left!* and *where's he going* and a great deal of *good morning, friend*. Somewhere in it, she was almost sure, very low down, there was a pale line that had said nothing at all since a shadow stood up in the corner last night.

She said it out loud. She had decided in the night that she would, because she had never in her life done anything important quietly, and she wasn't going to start now.

"Mothlight," she said. "Are you my mum?"

The scroll stopped.

She had never seen it stop. Not once, in seven years, not in her sleep, not when the bridge was falling, not on the bank of the mill-pond. It always moved; even on a dull night it crawled. Now there was nothing coming up the edge of her sight at all. Seventeen hundred powers, great and small and brass, and every one of them holding still, the way a room does when somebody drops a glass.

Hal put his bowl down on the floor very carefully.

It went on so long she began to be afraid. She thought: I was wrong. I was wrong, and she's only a moth, and they're all going to be so kind to me. She opened her mouth to say *never mind*.

A line came up. Pale. Shell-silver. Slow, as if every letter cost something.

*Mothlight: hello, little kettle*

Bry made a noise. She didn't know what it was. It wasn't a word. She put both hands over her mouth and the line sat there, alone in the scroll, and nobody wrote anything under it, and she read it again and again and again.

*Mothlight: i'm sorry. i'm slow at this. it goes when i try to hold it*

"That's all right," Bry said through her hands. "That's all right, take — take as long as you — Mum. *Mum.*"

*Mothlight: you used to whistle. when you were cross. like the kettle on the hob, you went red and you whistled. so that was what i called you*

"I know." She was laughing. She was crying, too, it turned out, in a way she hadn't done since she was twelve. "I know, you said it was the only thing in the house that never burnt."

*Mothlight: everything else burnt*

*Mothlight: the toast. i burnt the toast every morning and you ate it and said it was nice. you were a liar. a very kind liar*

*Mothlight: i saw you burn toast at the gull once. through the window. before i knew. that's when i knew i was someone's*

"I still burn it," Bry said. "Every day. I do it on purpose now. I didn't know I was doing it on purpose until just this minute."

Hal had taken his spectacles off and was polishing them on his blanket, steadily, with his head down.

*Mothlight: there's a lot i don't have any more. i'm sorry. i have songs. i have all of the songs. i have the toast and the curtain with the boats on it that you slept behind, and the drip by the window, and the stairs. i don't have the stairs right. were there nine?*

"Eleven."

*Mothlight: eleven. i'll keep that*

*Mothlight: i don't have my own face. i had it in the cage for a long time and then one day i didn't. i don't have what i was called, mostly. i had to think very hard to keep it. i kept you instead, mostly. i hope that's all right*

"It's all right," said Bry, and couldn't say anything else for a while.

The scroll was still empty except for the silver. Even Gorehound, who had never once in seven years held his tongue for anything, held it.

"Why didn't you say?" she managed at last. "All summer. You were *there*. You asked me if I'd eaten."

*Mothlight: i didn't know if i was her. i only knew the songs, and then the toast. you don't know what it is, being thin. you're not sure you're anyone. i was afraid if i said it and i was wrong you'd have to be kind to me*

Bry laughed, wet and helpless, because that was exactly it.

*Mothlight: and then the crow came*

"I know," said Bry. "I know. He told me. He told me you went with him."

*Mothlight: i want you to hear it from me*

*Mothlight: i wasn't taken, little kettle. i mean, i was. but i went. she'd have had your voice instead. she said so, through the mirror. she said a child's voice would do. so i went, so she wouldn't take you*

*Mothlight: you weren't left. you were never left. not for a man, not for anything. i want you to have that. i want you to keep it even if i go thin all the way*

Bry bent over until her forehead was nearly on her knees. The fire was hot on one side of her face. She thought of the garret, the curtain, eleven stairs, the note on the table under the cracked mirror, and the twelve years she had spent hating the woman who wrote it, carefully, every day, the way you keep a fire in.

"The note," she said. "Don't look for me. Be loud. Why did you—why *that*? Why not just tell me?"

*Mothlight: because if i told you, you'd have been afraid. you'd have spent your whole life afraid of the hill and the queen and every mirror. i'd rather you were angry. angry is loud. angry gets up in the morning*

*Mothlight: be loud, i said. and you were*

*Mothlight: that's how i found you. in the gallery it's all dark and murmuring and you were the loudest thing in it. a window with a girl in it, singing her head off and arguing with a hand*

*Saint Ambry's Left Hand: I resent that.*

Bry laughed so hard it hurt.

And then, as if that small dry gold voice had pulled a stopper, the scroll came back all at once. It came up in a flood: hearts, and *oh bry*, and *MUM*, and *she found you*, and the Nine Wet Gentlemen all weeping at once, *we are not crying, Abel is crying*, and Lantern-in-the-Fen writing *go left go left go left* over and over as if that meant something kinder than it usually did, and a single grey lowercase line she had not seen in nearly a month:

*Grandmother Cinder: there you are*

And through all of it, brass. Hundreds of brass hearts, hundreds of *so happy for you, friend*, *what a moment, friend*, the warm coins landing in her one after another and sinking in. Thimble's orange began to hush them, one at a time, a dozen, two dozen.

"No," said Bry. "Leave it, Thimble. I'll do it."

She'd never done it herself. It was Thimble's work; it had always been Thimble's. She put her hand out flat over the fire, the way she had over the salt cellar in Crake, and thought, as hard as she had ever thought anything: *Not you. Not today. This isn't yours.*

It was like putting her shoulder to a door with a crowd on the other side. It hurt in her teeth, in the bones of her wrist. One by one, then by tens, the brass voices in the scroll went grey and still. She couldn't stop their sparks. She couldn't make them leave. But she could make them be quiet while her mother was talking, and she did, every one, until the sweat ran down her back and the scroll was hearts and orange and green-black and gold and grey and silver and nothing else.

She sat back, breathing hard.

*Thimble: well done*

*Mothlight: you got strong*

"I got loud," said Bry.

---

It was some time later, when the fire had gone down and Hal had fallen asleep sitting up with his chin on his chest, that she asked about Corvin.

*Mothlight: he used to come into the hall to report. she likes to be sung to while she listens to other things. so i sang*

*Mothlight: after, when she'd gone, he would stay. by the cage. he never said anything. but he listened. every time. the whole of the song, to the end*

*Mothlight: eleven years. nobody else ever listened to the end*

Bry looked at the door, where the snow had drifted against the boards.

"Do you hate him?"

*Mothlight: i was afraid of him. i'm less afraid now. he came back for you*

*Mothlight: i know his name, you know. his real one. she said it to me once, to show me what she owned. i've kept it, like the stairs*

Bry went very still. "Tell me."

*Mothlight: no, love. it isn't mine to give*

*Mothlight: it's his to want. and he doesn't want it yet. he wants you safe more than he wants his name. when that changes, ask me again*

---

When Corvin came back it was nearly dark and he was carrying an armful of wood they didn't need. He stopped in the doorway with snow on his shoulders. The silver went out of the scroll, gently, the way a moth folds up.

Bry was sitting by the fire with the guestbook open on her knees. She had turned back through the autumn's pages to June, to a line in her own untidy hand: *Mothlight. Silver. Asks a lot of questions.* She had crossed out nothing. Underneath it, very carefully, she had written: *(Mum.)* And under that, because her mother had asked her to, so she would not lose it: *Hesper Kettle. Likes: songs. Burnt toast. Eleven stairs.*

She looked up at Corvin.

"She says you used to stay and listen," she said. "After the Queen had gone. To the end of the song."

He set the wood down by the door. For a moment he didn't look like anyone's crow at all.

"Every time," he said.

She held out the other half of the morning's toast. It had gone cold and it was very burnt. He came and took it and sat down on his side of the fire, and ate it.
