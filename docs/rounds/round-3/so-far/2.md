Merrit Foy was a clerk at the Exchange, where she stamped other people's pacts. On the night of 30 March, three nights before her birthday, she went to Gallows Cross with a stolen form. She also had chalk lines her friend Dov had copied from an old diagram of Wyck's. There she made an open call to any power willing to share. After a long silence a warm voice, Old Ember, answered, and then hundreds more did. Desperate for quiet, she told them to hush, Chat, and the name stuck.

Termin, god of a forgotten field boundary, explained the terms: they watch through her, they lend her boons, and their polls can compel her. Her flatmate Bet was furious that hundreds of powers had seen her too. On Merrit's birthday her mother, Agathe, took her to the flooded chapel where Agathe had been bound to the Undertow. The Undertow's cold found Merrit already taken. Terrified, Agathe told the price aloud: the Undertow saved Merrit from a fever at a week old, and it will take that life back at the Midsummer tide. That evening a silent patron, Nobody-in-Particular, joined.

The auditor Ilse Brannock registered the pact but said she would recommend voiding it. The Registrar, Hesper Quayle, offered to have a Throne sponsor it. Merrit refused and was dismissed from her clerkship.

Merrit grew her chorus from dispossessed things, like the bathhouse spirit Lady Calidarium. When a river wraith attacked her, gentle Saltlick died in its way. A generous new patron, Benefice, arrived, and Nobody-in-Particular spoke for the first time: "Careful." At the Spring Lists, Merrit was losing to Corwin Sallow, gilt-collared mark of the Throne called the Hound. She let Benefice take the reins and won. Chat swelled past six hundred, though Termin noticed seats being sold to Benefice. As her fame faded, Merrit paid two toughs to ambush her for show. Ilse watched the chorus save a child and told Merrit about her sister Margit, used up by a Throne. Wyck's papers, smuggled out by Dov, held a clause reaching back through every pact made under a fraudulent one. Quayle dismissed Dov. Then a patron called Mislaid found sixty-two hollow seats, all sold to Benefice.

Agathe begged Merrit to submit at Midsummer, and Merrit refused. Bet moved out, hurt by the lie about the ambush. Merrit nursed Corwin, whose Hound had nearly killed him for losing.

Quayle had Merrit locked in the lightless Grey Room. Chat drained away while Benefice bought the seats of those leaving. Spent, Merrit told them the truth, including the faked ambush and a frost in April her mother once mentioned. Chat began talking to each other as a people, and Old Ember fell silent. Ilse resigned as auditor to become Merrit's advocate and freed her after five days. The hearing is set for Midsummer Eve. Benefice then posted a binding poll, naming the Undertow, that Merrit submit at the tide.

Merrit, Dov, Ilse, Bet and Mislaid broke into Quayle's office at night. They found a ledger of seat sales paid for in Undertow essence and the Undertow's promise of a pact to Quayle. They also found Dr Amsel's casebook from Merrit's infancy: frost on the cradle-rail, a smell of brine, a fever without cause. Merrit read it three times.
