On 4 April, at the Exchange, auditor Ilse Brannock questioned Merrit Foy, a clerk from down the corridor. Merrit had made a pact under a form no one had used in two hundred years. She had no single patron. Instead, hundreds of lesser powers watched through her eyes, and she called them Chat. Ilse registered the pact but said she would recommend voiding it. Merrit answered that then she would be dead by Midsummer.

The pact began on 30 March at Gallows Cross. Merrit used a stolen form and chalk geometry that the archivist Dov had copied from an old diagram by Wyck. First came Old Ember, a warm, dry voice, and then hundreds more. Among them were Termin, a boundary-god who keeps the rules, including binding polls that can compel her, and slow, fond Saltlick and the hungry Gnash. Her flatmate Bet was furious at being watched.

On Merrit's birthday her mother Agathe told her the truth. At a week old Merrit had a fever, and Agathe bargained with a Throne called the Undertow for her life, which is owed back at the Midsummer tide. Agathe tried to bind Merrit to it, but the pact already occupied her. That evening a silent patron, Nobody-in-Particular, took a seat.

The Registrar, Hesper Quayle, offered Merrit a Throne's protection. Merrit refused and was dismissed from her job. Saltlick died shielding her from a river-wraith. A generous new patron, Benefice, joined. Losing the Spring Lists to Corwin Sallow, whose Throne holds him by a gilt collar, Merrit let Benefice "hold the reins" and won. To keep Chat watching, she later paid two toughs to ambush her. Bet found out and eventually left. Termin and the finder-spirit Mislaid discovered dozens of seats that had been sold to Benefice and gone hollow, Gnash's among them. Dov smuggled out Wyck's papers, which revealed a fraud clause able to reach back through every pact made under a fraudulent one.

On Quayle's warrant Merrit was locked in the lightless Grey Room. Chat drained away while Benefice bought up the seats of those who left. At last she told Chat the truth, including the ambush and a frost in April that her mother mentioned once and never again. Chat became a people. Old Ember fell silent. Ilse resigned as auditor to act as Merrit's advocate, freed her, and secured a hearing for Midsummer Eve.

Benefice then posted a binding poll that Merrit submit to the Undertow. It falls due at noon on 3 June. Breaking into Quayle's office, the friends found a ledger of seat sales brokered for "U" and the Undertow's promise of a pact to Quayle. They also found Dr Amsel's casebook from Merrit's infancy: frost on the cradle-rail, a smell of brine, a fever without cause.

On 2 June Agathe arrived half-drowned, cast off by the Undertow, and revealed that she was Nobody-in-Particular. Agathe said she had been alone with the baby and the fire. Old Ember spoke at last: "I was the fire." Tonight, it said, light the grate.
