1. straight: Let me get this straight
   4 April. An interview room at the Exchange. Ilse Brannock reads a foxed form XIV-c that no one has filed in two hundred years and says the line: a warlock with hundreds of patrons watching at once, whom she calls Chat? Merrit, whose own clerk's desk is three doors down the corridor, answers while Chat comment in her head (they think Ilse is wonderful; Gnash wants her to bite her) and laughs at the wrong moments. She registers the pact on the spot, which the Charter allows. Ilse says she will recommend voiding it. Merrit: then I'm dead by Midsummer. Ilse doesn't know what to say to that, and neither does the reader.
2. gallows-cross: Gallows Cross
   The night of 30 March, three nights before her birthday. Midnight at Gallows Cross, the stolen form, Dov's chalk geometry copied from Wyck's diagram, the formula of the open call to any power who will share. Silence long enough for Merrit to start laughing at herself. Then a warm, dry voice says 'Well, there you are' (Old Ember), and then hundreds at once, a room-full of voices that won't stop. Termin demands to read the terms. Saltlick wants to know what grass feels like through her feet. The Drowned Choir sing. They name stewards. At three in the morning, desperate for one minute of silence, she tells them to hush, Chat, and the name sticks.
3. eel: The pickled eel
   The next morning in the Tallowgate flat. Termin introduces himself (god of a boundary between two fields built over by tanneries two hundred years ago, now a step in a tannery yard) and lays out the terms: the window, the quorum, boons, polls. Gnash calls a poll that she eat an eel from Nan Gossage's stall in Gutter Market; it carries, and she learns in the market what it is to be compelled by a vote. Bet, discovering that three hundred powers watched her friend wash this morning, and therefore saw her too, hits the ceiling. Some of Chat leave, bored by the terms. Comedy, with the rule laid down.
4. drowned-chapel: The drowned chapel
   2 April, dawn, her birthday. Agathe brings her to the half-flooded chapel at the river stairs, where Agathe was bound, and Lisette before her. Merrit lets her mother get as far as the words. The Undertow's cold comes up the steps and breaks on her like a wave on a sea wall: occupied. Agathe understands, and her fury is terror. She tells the price aloud for Chat to hear: a fever at a week old, a life given, a life owed back at the Midsummer tide. Chat, who have never seen anything like it, surge. That evening a quiet new patron called Nobody-in-Particular takes a seat and says nothing at all.
5. registrar: The Registrar's offer
   5 April. Back at the Exchange after the audit, Hesper Quayle receives Merrit in her office with tea and silk. She offers to regularise the pact: a Throne could sponsor the seats and protect her. Merrit, who has stamped a thousand forms that began this way, refuses. Quayle dismisses her from the clerkship with real regret in her voice. Dov catches her on the archive stairs and tells her to be careful; Quayle took a lamp down to the flooded sub-archive last night. The reader is told nothing of who Quayle met.
6. calidarium: The Old Calidarium
   8 April. Mother Sorrel's first job: a haunting at the closed bathhouse in the Stews. Steam that scalds, tiles that crack, a voice in the pipes. Chat love it. The ghost is a starving bathhouse spirit; Gnash calls a poll to burn it out, Hollin argues, and the poll fails by eleven. Merrit offers the spirit a seat instead of an exorcism. Lady Calidarium joins and her first boon is the best bath of Merrit's life, watched by three hundred and fifty. Merrit's method is born: she grows the chorus out of the dispossessed.
7. hounds-tooth: The Hound's Tooth
   10 April. At the warlocks' tavern Corwin Sallow, the Exchange's golden boy, gilt at the throat, mocks her as a warlock run by committee. Chat feed her lines and she roasts him in front of the room. He challenges her to the Spring Lists. She accepts because Chat are chanting. Afterward, alone, Corwin touches his collar as if it hurt.
8. tide-wraith-scene: Drain-water
   12 April, two in the morning. The cellar drain of the chandler's shop breathes out a wraith of river-water and it comes up the stairs. Chat, half of them screaming, pool boons; the flat fills with borrowed lights and old weathers; Merrit fights for the first time with hundreds. The wraith lunges for her heart, and Saltlick, slow, fond Saltlick, is in the way. Then the wraith is gone and so is Saltlick, quiet. Twenty leave in fright. Merrit, kneeling in salt water, understands they can die for her.
9. wake: Saltlick's wake
   13 April. Chat hold a wake, a cacophony of memory for a salt-lick on a vanished common; Merrit puts her tongue to a block of salt from the chandler's because Chat asked. A new patron takes a seat: Benefice, warm, witty, generous, and gives a boon so great her hands glow for an hour. Chat are dazzled. Nobody-in-Particular speaks for the first time: 'Careful.' Nobody asks it why.
10. lists: The Spring Lists
   20 April. The city on the Lists' stands; Corwin, brilliant, the Hound's gold pouring off him; Merrit, fighting with three hundred voices calling moves, losing badly. Benefice offers more, and she takes it. She wins in a burst of borrowed power that makes the crowd scream. Corwin is down on the sand, and the gilt at his throat tightens before everyone. Chat surge past six hundred. Termin, counting, notes that eleven of the newcomers' seats were sold to Benefice within the hour.
11. tallowgate-ambush: The ambush in Tallowgate
   26 April. A week of fame, and the surge is fading; she can feel the power draining with their boredom. Merrit pays two of Sorrel's toughs to jump her in an alley. Chat roar; the count tops six hundred and eighty. She tells them she doesn't know who sent them. Old Ember, who saw the coins change hands through her own eyes, says nothing. At home Bet, who heard it from the toughs, asks her who she thinks she's performing for.
12. dark-hours: The dark hours
   28 April. Merrit calls a poll for one hour a day unwatched. Termin reads it; it fails by hundreds. Quell, small and serious, tries to explain what the window is to a god who exists only when seen: before you, I was a chalk mark that went out every time it rained. Merrit, furious and ashamed, goes to the bath and weeps with six hundred and fifty watching. Then Hollin says, gently, look away, all of you, and some of them do; she feels the room thin. Nobody-in-Particular is among those who look away.
13. stews-warding-scene: The cellar in the Stews
   3 May. Ilse shadows Merrit on a warding job in the Stews, notebook out. A tenement cellar gives way with a child in it; the chorus pull him out, every Least spending something, a boundary-stone's stubbornness, a lost thing's luck, a saint's mercy. Ilse sees what a commons is for. Afterwards, on the steps, in her flat voice, she tells Merrit about her sister Margit, used up by a Throne at twenty-three and recorded as fair use. She does not change her recommendation, yet.
14. wyck: The Wyck papers
   6 May. Dov smuggles Wyck's papers out of the deep archive in a laundry bag. They read in the flat by candle while Chat crowd behind her eyes to see. Clause Fourteen was meant to give the Least standing in the Court of Seals, so they could testify against Thrones for the first time. Wyck's margin: a seat is held by watching. And the fraud clause, Wyck's, written to reach back through every pact made under a fraudulent one. Dov tells her about his well-god and the pebbles. The next day Quayle dismisses him after forty-one years.
15. ring: The widow's ring
   9 May. A sailor's widow wants his ring back from the river. Mislaid finds it in an eel-trap; then Mislaid, giddy, keeps finding: seats in the chorus that have gone hollow, sold, nobody watching through them. Gnash is gone; he sold his seat to Benefice. Chat turn on the absent Gnash and decide he has been eating the little ones. Termin counts sixty-two hollow seats, all Benefice's, and goes quiet.
16. agathe-door: Agathe at the door
   12 May, ten at night. Agathe comes alone up the chandler's stairs, without the Undertow's cold around her for once, and asks to sit. She begs Merrit to submit at the Midsummer tide: she believes the Undertow will take back the life it gave, and she has spent twenty-five years living inside that price so that her daughter could live. For an hour they are almost mother and daughter. Agathe calls Chat a mob of starving things. Merrit refuses. Agathe goes. Nobody-in-Particular is absent from the chorus the whole hour, which Termin notes.
17. bet-goes: Bet goes
   14 May. Bet packs. She can't live in a flat with six hundred strangers behind her friend's eyes, and she can't stand the lie about the ambush, which Merrit still hasn't told them. She says she is lonelier now than before the pact. She leaves her lamplighter's spare chalk on the table, and Quell asks Merrit to keep it.
18. gutter: The gutter
   16 May. Merrit finds Corwin half-dead in a Tallowgate gutter, the collar biting, the Hound having fed on him for losing the Lists. Hollin and Calidarium spend themselves to heal him. In the flat, wrapped in Bet's abandoned blanket, he asks how the commons works, whether he could have one; the answer is no while the collar holds. He is frightened of his own patron for the first time aloud.
19. arrest-scene: The warrant
   20 May. Exchange wardens at the flat at dawn with Quayle's warrant: an unlicensed danger, held pending hearing. Ilse arrives running, cites the Charter, and is overruled by her Registrar in front of the wardens. Merrit is walked down Seal Street, below the waterline, to the Grey Room. The door closes and the world goes out.
20. grey: The Grey Room
   20 to 22 May. Nothing: no light, no sound, the air at blood heat. Merrit performs into the dark: jokes, songs, the Lists told again, push-ups counted aloud. Chat leave in dozens, then hundreds; she can feel them go like warmth from a hand. Termin calls the count each hour: three hundred, two hundred, one hundred and sixty. And something Termin can't account for: Benefice is buying the seats of those leaving, holding the count up. Why would anyone want this pact to live?
21. truth: What she tells them in the dark
   23 May. Spent, she stops performing. In the dark she tells them the truth: the clerkship, stamping other people's bargains to find her own way out; her mother at the cradle; the frost in April her mother mentioned once and never again; the ambush she paid for. Silence. Then Chat start talking, to her and then to each other: Saltlick's common, Calidarium's opening day, the Choir's last peal, Quell's first rain. For the first time they are not an audience but a people. The leaving stops; some come back. When she says the word frost, Old Ember goes silent and stays silent.
22. release-scene: Five days
   25 May. Ilse opens the Grey Room door with the Charter in her hand: no warlock held without hearing past five days. She has resigned as auditor to stand as Merrit's advocate. The hearing is set for Midsummer Eve before the Court of Seals, the day before the tide. Quayle, watching them climb into the light, smiles: she has what she needs. Merrit comes out to three hundred and five seats as the returning patrons find her again, and doesn't know yet that nearly a third of them are hollow.
23. binding-poll: The binding poll
   27 May. Benefice posts a binding poll on Merrit's person: Merrit Foy shall submit to the binding of the Undertow at the Midsummer tide. The name of the Throne in the poll is the first time Benefice has shown its hand. It holds a hundred and ninety of four hundred seats; Termin rules seven days' notice. Chat in uproar; Mislaid can find a lost thimble but not the hand that holds the hollow seats; Merrit understands she has seven days to prove who sold them.
24. seal-street: Seal Street by night
   1 June, two in the morning. Dov knows the archive; Ilse has her keys still; Bet, back for this, keeps watch under a lamp she's left unlit; Mislaid finds. In Quayle's sealed office they find the seat-ledger, every sale brokered for 'U' in Undertow essence, and, pinned in its cover, the Undertow's promise to Quayle of the pact it refused her at twenty-five. And in the Foy family file Dr Amsel's casebook: frost on the cradle-rail in April, a smell of brine, fever without cause. Merrit reads it three times.
25. nobody-unmasked: Nobody-in-Particular
   2 June, morning. Agathe on the chandler's stairs, salt in her lungs, half drowned: the Undertow found the sliver of its power she had been diverting since the birthday. She has been Nobody-in-Particular all along, the steady small boons, the 'Careful', the looking away in the bath. Merrit puts the physician's note in her hands.
26. ember-tells: What the hearth saw
   2 June, night. At the cold grate of the flat, Old Ember speaks at last, to Merrit and to Agathe both, slowly, as if pulling it up from deep water: the cold that came up through the nursery floor from the drowned well, the frost on the cradle, the night she poured herself into the room, the voice at dawn and the child warm the moment Agathe said yes. She has watched over the child of her house ever since, from the ruin and then the streets; that is why she answered first. Agathe breaks. Merrit sees the pattern: the wraith, then Benefice; the frost, then the cure.
27. expulsion: Expulsion
   3 June. At sunrise Termin audits the seats as the terms require: three hundred and thirty, Benefice holding a hundred and seventy-two, more than half. At noon, before the count, Termin, with the ledger and Mislaid's finding, moves to expel Benefice: one power holding seats it does not watch from, against Wyck's note. Ember seconds as steward. Benefice laughs and its seats go out like lamps: a hundred and fifty-eight. Then the Undertow's cold comes through the chorus in revenge and forty more flee. A hundred and eighteen. Quell, tiny, holds the rest by talking, as Merrit did in the dark. Sunset to sunset; they hold.
28. walking: Walking the city
   8 to 18 June. Merrit walks Cantle with her window open, listening for forgotten things: a doorknocker god on a boarded house, a weathervane, the god of a ferry-stair, the drowned of the lower parishes murmuring under the cobbles. She tells each the truth about what a seat costs and what it gives. Corwin walks with her, collar and all; Bet chalks Quell's mark on every corner of Gannet Street. The count passes a thousand. Gnash watches from a pub sign and will not come down.
29. last-evening: Ember's last evening
   19 June. Ember explains: to testify before the Court of Seals a power must swear on its essence, and she has very little left. Merrit forbids it. Ember, steward, calls a poll on herself, and Chat carry it for her, fourteen hundred voices, the first poll that has ever been a gift. They sit by the cold grate, and Ember tells her what the hearth at Sallowmarsh was like in winter.
30. court: The Court of Seals
   20 June, Midsummer Eve. Quayle, still Registrar, argues the clause is a dead letter and seats are property, rightly sold. Ilse answers with the Charter, Wyck's note, the ledger. Then the first of the Least ever to testify before the Court: Old Ember, speaking through Merrit's mouth in an old woman's voice, swears on her essence and tells the Court what came up through the floor.
31. ruling-scene: Ruling
   20 June, evening. The Court finds the birth-bargain obtained by fraud. Void, and every pact made under it back to Odile Foy in 1052: Merrit owes nothing; Agathe, in the gallery, feels the Undertow's cold go out of her for the first time since she was twenty-five. The clause stands; a seat is held by watching. The ledger is read into the record and Quayle is stripped of her office. At the end of her testimony, still in Merrit's mouth, Ember says 'there you are' again, and goes quiet. The count falls by one.
32. flood: The Midsummer tide
   21 June, one in the morning. Robbed of the Foy line and the chorus, the Undertow comes up the Brack at the height of the tide and floods the lower town to the steps of Seal Street. Merrit fights with fourteen hundred and it is not enough. The Drowned Choir, under the water, ring the bell of St Ide's, which hasn't sounded in a century, and the drowned districts' Least rise to it in thousands; Gnash comes back with them, unpaid. Agathe, powerless now, rows a skiff through Tallowgate. The Hound orders Corwin to leave Merrit to drown; he pulls her out, and the collar falls off his throat into the water.
33. witness: Witness
   21 June, dawn, the river mouth. Fighting won't end it. Merrit asks the thousands to look: not through her at the world, but with her at the Undertow, and the drowned Least who remember the marsh tell what it was. Under all that attention it is seen for what it began as, one hungry tidepool, and it shrinks back down the Brack, diminished, and the water goes out. Merrit understands what watching is for.
34. hearth: The hearthstone
   1 July. Merrit, Agathe and Bet dig the Sallowmarsh hearthstone out of the drainage works and carry it to the Tallowgate flat. Quell is named steward with Termin. Chat call a poll, unasked, to give Merrit one hour a day unwatched; it carries by thousands. She spends the first hour with Bet, and the fire they light on the stone. At the end of the hour a tiny new hearth-spirit is there in the grate, not Ember, not remembering her, and asks to take a seat. They call it Kindle.
35. say-hi: Say hi, Chat
   15 July, midnight, Gallows Cross. Corwin, collarless, powerless, with a new form XIV-c Ilse has had printed by the hundred. He reads it twice and looks at Merrit: wait, let me get this straight, hundreds of them, all watching, and I call them Chat? Call yours whatever you like, she says. Then, to the thousands behind her eyes: Say hi, Chat. They do, all at once.
