Brannoc, a knight hired by the bridge wardens to clear the Marle Bridge cistern, found a young woman already down there, haggling with the dark over the price of a firebolt. Together they killed the ghoul. She was Kit, a warlock with no single patron. Instead hundreds of lesser powers watch her through what she calls the Window. She calls them Chat, and they pay in tithes for the spells and spectacle they want. Among them are gentle Silt, the first; Gristle, who wants blood; Pennywhistle, always joking; and quiet Lampwick. Kit lives every moment as a performance for them. Brannoc told her his god, Iver, had fallen silent, and about a last vision. She mocked it and left him the bill.

Kit counts her gallery every dawn. Lately the number has risen, but more and more names are grey: they watch and never tithe or speak. When wardens under Ilse Marrable cornered her for holding an unlicensed pact, Brannoc stepped in under an old right and claimed her as his to protect after dark. Ilse warned her about Hollin the Many-Eyed. Ysolde, a grey-haired scholar who keeps banned books, explained the danger: under the law of majority holding, whoever holds more than half a gallery holds the pact. Someone was buying Kit's patrons.

The buyer is Corvin Thale, a courteous, tired factor bound to the Exchequer, who keeps an exact ledger of his purchases and his own damnation. He called on Kit and told her Maud was alive and indentured in the Counting House, which Kit hadn't known. He offered Maud's freedom and a quiet head in exchange for the pact, and left a pale coin to spend at a crossroads at midnight. Kit threw him out. Her plan to outgrow him by putting on a public show against the Sinks weir-wyrm went wrong: a boy, Tobin, was badly hurt. His grandmother Nan Reave took Kit's money and refused her apology.

Kit, Brannoc and Ysolde went down the Marle by barge toward Saltcombe to find Hollin's testament. At Orrin Bridge the grey names withheld their power all at once. Brannoc went into the river in his mail, and Kit pulled him out with no magic at all. While he was feverish she told him how her pact began: alone in the Sump at fourteen, nine days in the dark. He asked why she had been alone, and she couldn't answer. Ysolde admitted Corvin was her former student, who took the pact to pay her debts. Kit kept her on anyway.

Pennywhistle, fading and forgotten, quietly sold itself to Corvin. At Wending Fair, Kit busked with no magic, and even the silent watchers tithed. Now 196 of her 489 patrons are grey, forty-nine short of a majority, and Pennywhistle is among them. Gristle refused Corvin openly. At drowned Saltcombe, Kit read Hollin's testament: Hollin shut her eyes to hide, starved her patrons, and they sold her. *Do not shut your eyes.* Kit took Hollin's roll of three hundred names. Then Corvin came with the abbey's dead clerks. Lampwick's single tithe lit Brannoc's shield for the first time in three years, and he held the gate. To end the fight, Ysolde gave herself up to Corvin as the tide came in.
