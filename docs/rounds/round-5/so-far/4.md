Brannoc is a paladin whose god has been silent for three years. While clearing the cistern under Marle Bridge, he met Kit, a busker and warlock. Her power comes from hundreds of small, faded gods who watch her through her Window and pay her in tithes for spells and spectacle, and she calls them Chat. Among them are gentle Silt, once a ford of the Marle; cruel Gristle; joking Pennywhistle; and quiet Lampwick, who tithes one mote during every one of Brannoc's prayers.

Kit's pact is illegal under the Concordance, the law written because of Hollin the Many-Eyed. Brannoc protected her from the warden Ilse Marrable under an old right of escort. The scholar Ysolde explained the law of majority holding: whoever owns more than half a warlock's patrons owns the warlock. Kit's grey names, patrons who watch but never speak, were being bought by Corvin Thale. He is factor to the Exchequer, a Throne that keeps accounts, and he took its pact to pay Ysolde's debts.

Kit staged a reckless fight with the weir wyrm in front of a crowd, and it left the boy Tobin Reave with a broken arm. Corvin offered to free Kit's mother Maud, who is alive and indentured, if Kit would sell the pact, and he left her a pale coin. Kit refused. Pennywhistle, fading because children no longer sing its rhyme, secretly sold itself.

At the drowned abbey of Saltcombe, Ysolde admitted that Corvin had been her student. Hollin's testament showed that Hollin had shut her eyes on her patrons, starved them, and been sold. Kit took a roll of three hundred names belonging to Hollin's chorus, who are now hollow clerks. When Corvin came, Lampwick's tithe lit Brannoc's shield, and Ysolde went with Corvin to end the fight. That night Kit closed the Window to think in private and kissed Brannoc. In the dark, eighty-seven patrons faded, and Kit swore never to close it again.

Later, in tithes only Kit could read, Lampwick told her it is Brannoc's god, foreclosed by the Exchequer, and that it would be found if he named it. She has kept the secret.

Kit spent the coin to enter the Exchequer with the Window open. She found Maud and learned that Maud had pledged Kit as surety before running. She also read her bought patrons' contracts, each bound by a true name it had forgotten. Maud signed herself surety for Kit's exit.

With exactly half her patrons bought, Kit held the Naming on Lampnight in Iver's Square. The lamps were lit by hand, crowds came from the Sinks, and Ilse's wardens stood at every entry. Kit told her patrons' stories by their true names, skipping Lampwick, and as the crowd said the names back, the grey names bought themselves free. Pennywhistle had been sent to make the final sale, but it bought itself back and sent Kit a private note. When the Exchequer's clerks marched in, Kit refused Gristle's offer of blood and read them their own names from Hollin's roll. They stopped and listened, and some wept salt.
