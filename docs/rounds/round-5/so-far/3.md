Brannoc, a paladin, was hired by the bridge wardens to clear the Marle Bridge cistern. Down there he found Kit, a young warlock who has no single patron but hundreds of lesser ones: small, fading gods who watch through her eyes, pay tithes for the spells and moments they want, and talk on her scroll. She calls them Chat, her mum's word for the cheap seats. Among them are Silt, once a ford of the Marle; Gristle, who wants blood; Pennywhistle, who jokes about everything; and Lampwick, who barely speaks and tithes a single mote at a time.

Brannoc's god, Iver, has been silent for three years. Stubborn about his oath, Brannoc followed Kit, and when Ilse Marrable's wardens cornered her for holding an unlicensed pact, he claimed the old right to walk her home after dusk. Ilse let her go but warned her about Hollin the Many-Eyed. Ysolde, a scholar of banned books, explained that whoever holds more than half of a pact's patrons holds the pact. Many of Kit's watchers had gone grey, meaning they had been bought.

The buyer is Corvin Thale, a courteous, tired factor of the House of Pale Coin who works for the Exchequer. Kit tried to outgrow him with a public fight against the weir wyrm in the Sinks. She took Chat's money for a grander finish, the weir-gate burst, and a boy named Tobin was badly hurt. Corvin then called on her. Her mother, Maud, is alive and indentured in the Counting House, he said, and would go free if Kit sold him the pact. He left her a pale coin to spend at a midnight crossroads.

Kit, Brannoc and Ysolde went downriver by barge toward Saltcombe and Hollin's testament. Ysolde admitted that Corvin was her student and had taken the Exchequer's pact to pay her debts. Pennywhistle, fading, secretly sold itself to him.

Hollin's testament, in Saltcombe's drowned abbey, says she shut her eyes to hide from her patrons, starved them, and was sold by them for bread. Its warning is: do not shut your eyes. Corvin arrived with the Exchequer's writ and the abbey's dead clerks rose. Lampwick tithed hugely, and Brannoc's lamp-shield lit for the first time in three years. Ysolde went with Corvin so the fight would stop.

That night Kit closed the Window anyway, to think in private, and kissed Brannoc. Silt held the gallery together in the dark. When Kit reopened it, eighty-seven were gone, and Corvin held two hundred of four hundred and two, two short of a majority. She swore never to close it again. Now, three weeks up the Marle by towpath, Brannoc has admitted he has been praying into nothing, and Kit is thinking about Lampwick's motes.
