# Part 1: blind judgments

I read the rubric and all eight packets in full. Each pair is judged only on what is on the page. The two packets in each pair are formatted differently: one is mostly prose, the other a structured list of wants, opposition and "puts pressure on" lines. I haven't let that sway the judgments, except where the format made the story harder or easier to read.

---

## Thukker, pair 1

### 1. Keep developing

**A.** *Lot Nine* has a single question that runs through every part: who has been keeping Tavik, and will he let himself be finished? After rework 1, Tavik wants the opposite of what Kaari wants. She is trying to undo what was done to her sister, and he wants it done to himself ("He doesn't tell her he's going there to become what took her sister"). That gives the midpoint a real turn: Tavik takes Tenna's side and Kaari understands why he came. It also turns both climax offers (Asami's "take her place as pilot" and the Auditor's "a hull of your own with no governor") into real temptations. The relay is a well-built device: it carries Hesk's reports, Asami's payments and the governor's commands, so cutting it frees Tenna, ends the watching and ends any chance of finishing Tavik, all at once.

**B** has good parts too: Tavik killing his foster brother Rask with his mind, the knot-tattoo counting songs that keep a pilot steady, the graveyard of half-minds, and Mira choosing to stay a Titan. But its central want ("Master what he is", then "Have the socket cut out") ties into the other characters less. The CONCORD buyer reveal and Velan's books feel like an add-on, and the climax ("Mira won't fire on their mother; Tavik turns the guns on the yard") is only sketched.

I'd rather keep developing **A**.

### 2. Open questions

**A** settles its big questions early and well: what Tavik is (Lot Nine, bought from slavers), why he left, what the Jove want, and the thread through everything (the relay). It deliberately keeps one thing ambiguous: the Auditor "kept him unfinished as a mercy or as a control… He doesn't know which, either." That ambiguity carries weight in the story rather than just being vague. The ending is "left open on purpose", with another ship-mind out in the dark. A doesn't show competing candidates being weighed, but what it settled fits together.

**B** lists its "Decided" answers clearly, and some of them do real work. "Did Mira, reaching for Tavik, cause the accident? No… Rask's death stays his" is a good answer: it refuses an easy way out of Tavik's guilt. "Why the same vault, the same night? Mira leaked to both" is neat. B also shows no alternatives being weighed. Rework 1 later dropped some of these decisions (the Mira/accident answer, the same-vault question among the reader's questions) without comment.

Both packets settle questions reasonably well; A's settled answers are more closely tied together.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A is better.** The change from "thinks Hesk sold him" to "was the ship for one minute, then found *Keep him out of hulls*" reaches the theme line, Tavik's fear, the reader's question ("who told Hesk to keep him out?"), the yard scene ("what he felt in the hauler, but caged"), the midpoint ("his envy cracks: being finished came with a governor"), both offers, the climax (he pulls out by choice) and the ending (he plugs into the *Dust Owed* for one hour and comes back). It also adds a new question for the reader ("Will he let himself be finished?").

What A missed: if he wants to be finished, it never says why he has "never let himself plug into" the hauler he rebuilt. Rework 2 then trims that to "has never plugged into", which loses the self-restraint. Kaari's job running logistics also disappears without comment.

B's rework 1 (leave to have the socket cut out) does reach Drekka's scene ("She asks him to stay. He'll come back… when the socket's gone"), the elders become his allies, the Archivist "could… and won't", the graveyard ("without his socket, only Mira could"), the second hull ("by choice"), and the ending, where he is offered the cure and keeps the socket. That is solid.

What B missed: the cut "Did Mira cause the accident?" answer and the same-vault question vanish without comment. And how the elders, whose want is "keep Tavik's danger away", come to accept him home with the socket still in is covered only by "Drekka has shamed the elders".

**Rework 2 (event moved in time): A is better, though both are good.** A moves Hesk's confession from the caravan visit to the final battle. It follows this through carefully:
- In part III Hesk now refuses to come and sends him off.
- Dass's logs half-answer the question in her place.
- The caravan arrives uninvited.
- The Auditor orders Hesk to pull Tavik out of the Titan, so the sockets grip from when he was nineteen comes round again.
- The ending adds "Hesk's haulers in it now, their pay gone with the relay".
- The reader's questions are updated.

A missed only small things: it never says how Hesk ends up aboard the Titan (the scene simply puts her there), and the armada now forms "without the caravan" without saying where its ships come from.

B moves Mira's fusion to twelve years earlier, at fourteen and dying. This reaches Haruka's stake ("else what she did to Mira was murder"), Ishu ("eight at Mira's funeral", "the girl at an empty grave"), the ledger ("payments outlive Mira's funeral by eleven years"), the graveyard ("one a year since Mira", which matches twelve wrecks), the mother's offer ("the sister she buried, grown into a body"), and Mira's refusal ("a ship longer than she was a girl"). The arithmetic checks out.

What B missed: "Mira… has slipped Lattice" is a new fact that is never explained. If she has been free for a long time, that changes Haruka's hunt and the "second hull" plot, and nothing addresses it.

### 4. The scene

**A ("The Hand")** is set at the exact point where both reworks meet. The hauler grip from when he was nineteen, the *Keep him out of hulls* order and the confession now placed at the climax all land in it: Hesk with her hand on the sockets, the Auditor counting down, and her choosing not to pull. "I was paid to keep him small… He's not small any more." The ending lines ("She'd have let me stay." / "I came out anyway." / "I know. I saw.") land the motivation change. It's controlled and moving.

**B ("The graveyard of half-minds")** reflects both reworks clearly: the Archivist's "*I could*", "the thing he'd crossed half the cluster to be rid of", "one a year", and "I had fourteen of them." The counting song, with Mira finishing "three for the brother… we carry home", is a lovely beat, and the sensory details (oranges, *ichi ni san*) are good. It has copy errors ("fourteen kilometres long,.", "like his own arm,.", and two speeches merged into one quotation), and the scene's turn ("He was still there at the end of it") is softer.

Both scenes reflect the reworked story. **A's is the better scene.**

### 5. Has what it needs

**A: yes.** Tavik wants to be finished; Kaari wants Tenna home; Tenna wants to be unleashed, not undone; Asami wants the sale; the Auditor wants the watching to go on; Hesk wants to keep him. There is opposition at every level. The reader's questions are tracked by act, and the stakes are both personal (his self, her sister) and outward (the Navy sale).

**B: yes, mostly.** Every character has an explicit want, a stake and an opponent, and the reader is waiting on six questions. The outer plot (CONCORD, Velan) is thinner than the family plot, and the final confrontation is a one-line outline.

**Preference: A. Confidence: medium.**

## Thukker, pair 2

### 1. Keep developing

**A** (Sev, Saisi, Kaeli and Kaaven) has a clear emotional core: Saisi wants her sister back "as she was", and Kaeli is out of pain for the first time and won't go back into a dying body. Kaaven is an unusually good antagonist. His capsuleer Titan pilot warped out and thousands died, so he wants "a Titan that cannot leave", and in the end his own fleet abandons him. After the reworks, Sev hates lies and is paired with an excellent liar. He also watches Rikkan, "a parent who made a deal over a child and called it love." The ending turns the premise's armada into a homecoming "paid for twice, and kept."

**B** (Hask, Aino, Kiri) has an excellent engine: the core is keyed to Hask, so Kiri burns out inside it, and "the armada is the cure": the linked fleet spreads her mind out, with Hask as the hub. That makes "build a small armada" the solution to the plot rather than decoration. Kiri's clock (lost words, months left) is strong. After the reworks, Hask sold the core himself and Aino's escape caused Kiri's swap, two matching debts that may be too neat.

I'd rather keep developing **A**, narrowly. Its people are more fully drawn and its central turn (Kaeli doesn't want saving) is set up better. B has the better mechanism, but its characters come across mainly through list entries.

### 2. Open questions

**A** opens by naming the premise's open questions ("Why he left… who the girl's family is… what his background is… are all open"). It answers each with a specific choice and keeps a useful "Still open" list. For example, whether Kaaven survives is weighed both ways, each tied to the rest of the story: a sequel hook if he lives, and "if he dies, it should be at the hands of his own crew's refusal, not the heroes'." After rework 2 it adds "Whether Haldra's arrival reads as rescue by chance. It must not: she paid for it." That is holding an undecided point in a useful way.

**B** only lists decisions. They are good ones ("How can Kiri survive a core not keyed to her? Spread her across a linked fleet") but nothing is held open or weighed. Rework 1 deletes three Decided entries (Kiri chose, Ondrai's fate, Hask's background) as a side effect.

A handles this better.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A is slightly better.** Sev now leaves in anger because his belonging was paid for. This reaches:
- his want ("belong to something nobody was paid to give him")
- Oveg's role (paid Haldra for reports)
- Lusse (found the payments)
- the "turn" paragraph
- scene 3 (he hates Saisi's lying)
- scene 4 (harder on Rikkan)
- the homecoming (both sides wronged)
- Oveg's confession ("sixteen years of reports, none true")
- the climax temptation ("whole, and belonging to nobody")
- the ending

What A missed: "none true" slightly contradicts "her reports grew shorter and less true every year." It also never says why the Jovian kept paying every year if the reports had stopped saying anything.

B's rework 1 (Hask sold the core himself) reaches scene 2 ("waits to be thanked. Orsa only says it was his"), scene 4 (his confession), scene 5 ("owing, nearly agrees"), scene 7 (renamed "What he owes"; "Nobody here buys a place"), the Saevel message ("Never salvage; given") and the ending ("kin, not debt").

What B missed: Orsa still "Opposes Hask: Buy back the core", but nothing on the page shows her opposing it. It also drops three Decided answers without comment. In the scene, Hask's reason for buying it back ("I thought somebody would put it in a museum") sits a little oddly beside "when the buyer proves to be State".

**Rework 2 (event moved in time): roughly even, A slightly ahead.** A moves the homecoming from the middle to the climax and follows it through:
- Sev now buys a mercenary fleet instead ("He is doing what Haldra did, and Saisi says so").
- Oveg's confession produces shame but not a return home, and a new question ("will he ever face her?").
- Lusse sells the armada's position to "one other buyer" (Haldra).
- The paid crews scatter.
- The Thukker arrive unasked, and Sev feels Haldra's hauler hurting through the leash.
- The face-to-face reckoning moves to the ending, and the open-questions list gains a guard against coincidence.

What A missed: the "gallivanting" stretch loses the caravan-as-fleet image, and the effect of that on the premise's armada isn't discussed.

B moves Aino's escape earlier, so that Kiri's swap is what ends the hunt for her. This reaches Aino's and Kiri's wants, the events list, scene 3 ("where Aino has stolen for a living since she ran"), scene 9 ("who thought she left owing nothing") and the scene itself (Kiri's lie that "They paid", and Aino's "in two years nobody had come after her").

What B missed: how swapping test records actually ends a hunt for a runaway is asserted rather than explained. It also doesn't say how Kiri's letters were reaching a sister who was in hiding.

### 4. The scene

**A ("The Cradle")** is the climax. It reflects both reworks: the paid crews jumping ("Proof… of what paid-for things were"), the Thukker arriving unasked, and Haldra's voice in the same shout as in the opening wreck-field. Kaeli's line "*Are you here to hold my leash, or to take my place?*" and Sev's "*Neither*" land well. Some passages explain the theme outright ("Who had been paid to keep him, and had taken the money…"), but the turn is clear and earned.

**B ("The voice in the hull")** reflects rework 1 (Hask's confession, "I sold the thing that's eating your sister") and rework 2 (Aino ran; Kiri covers with "They paid… You don't get to be the sensible one"). Its dialogue texture is excellent: "Little fish", the lost name, the corridor lights steadying when Hask steps through, and "Enough to get you home first." It ends on a strong forward question: the man who sold the box is now the only thing keeping her sister whole.

Both are good. B's is a slightly better-made scene: more subtext, less explanation. A's carries more weight because it is the climax.

### 5. Has what it needs

**A: yes.** Strong wants and opposition (Kaaven, Lusse, Oveg's offer against Kaeli's choice). Raised and answered questions are marked in the outline. The stakes are personal (Kaeli's selfhood, Sev's belonging) and outward (the Navy, the leash).

**B: yes.** Every character has a want, a stake, a method and an opponent. The reader's questions are tracked, and Kiri's burnout clock adds urgency. The ending ("Haatakan… withdraws") is a little easy for a director who has been hunting them all along.

**Preference: A. Confidence: low.**

## Magical girl therapist, pair 1

### 1. Keep developing

**A** (Noreen, Bridget, Dani, Teo, Marcus) has a strong, simple rule: the Bright goes to "whoever steps first between a Squall and someone smaller; adults hesitate to calculate; children don't." It mirrors two families: Noreen and Bridget then, Dani and Teo now. Teo "practises Dani's stance in the hallway mirror", a great image with real stakes. After rework 2, Halsey Street happens during the story, with Dani on call. The cost Noreen fears happens in front of her, and June becomes a living eleven-year-old Warden.

**B** (Harriet, Dani, Juno, Pell, Ines, Marisol) has a stated central contradiction: "Good therapy would heal Dani… healing Dani disarms the city." After rework 1 it adds a hidden agenda: Harriet takes Pell's money "meaning to cure the girl anyway", and Pell finds out. B has more voices in opposition: Pell, who is "no villain, just a guardian with old rules"; Ines, the supervisor ("You're not treating Dani. You're treating Marisol"); and Marisol, who carries her own guilt. Its ending costs more and is less clean ("Pell looks at Juno a moment too long… stays open on purpose").

I'd keep developing **B**, narrowly. It has more opposition and more questions in play, including "Will Pell find out what Harriet is doing?" and the lie Harriet tells Marisol at the end of the scene. A is tighter and more cleanly built, though, and the gap is small.

### 2. Open questions

**A's** "Decided" answers are strong and consistent ("What happens when Dani finally can't? Noreen gets in front of Teo first"). It doesn't show alternatives or keep anything open.

**B** explicitly weighs its main answer against alternatives: "This answer brings back the warm hands, gives 'nearest in kind' a second meaning, and makes Harriet's guilt specific. A former magical girl, or Pell letting the deaths happen, would both do less work." It also keeps a short, sensible "Still open" list (what Starling's power looks like; whether Harriet tells Ines everything) and a "Costs" section.

B holds its undecided points more usefully.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A slightly better.** A changes Noreen from the child who was hidden to the child who lit up first and froze. This reaches:
- Bridget's want ("Keep Noreen out of the Bright"; "Says aloud what neither has said in forty-eight years")
- Noreen's stake ("Dani becomes Noreen at six; Teo becomes Bridget")
- scene 2 (Noreen puts her pen down when Dani describes locking up)
- Bridget's line in the kitchen ("You lit up first. Then you locked, and I paid six years")
- the confession in scene 5
- the stairwell ("she holds")
- the coda ("she held it well")
- the Decided list

What A missed: "Noreen books herself a therapist" is dropped without comment, and the reader's question "why does Noreen flinch from it?" stays unchanged until rework 2.

B changes Harriet's guilt from curing Marisol to keeping her lit. This reaches:
- the shape paragraph
- Harriet's want and flaw ("she wants the cure for herself")
- Pell's line ("You did so well with the Reyes girl")
- Ines's question
- Marisol ("She isn't cured")
- the rule ("heals, or breaks")
- the Settled section
- scenes 2–8, and the costs

What B missed: the good question "Will Dani let go?" is deleted rather than adapted. Harriet's old flaw ("made a virtue of staying in the chair") goes with it, so "stands up out of the metaphorical chair" at the climax loses what it was paying off.

**Rework 2 (event moved in time): A is better.** A puts Halsey Street two weeks into the therapy and follows it through carefully:
- It replaces June with "Sade, sixteen" in Dani's earlier panic attack, because June can't have covered Dani before she got the Bright. Good catch.
- It dates Fenn's dismissal.
- The news is on in Bridget's kitchen.
- Marcus has the photographs on his desk and "Dani was on call."
- Scene 5 opens with Dani not sleeping "since Halsey Street."
- The terms say "June first."

What A missed: Marcus still wants Dani signed fit by Friday right after she locked and two people died. That is plausible given the shortage, but the story doesn't comment on it.

B moves Marisol's break to three nights before Carrow Street, so Harriet's refusal of the light caused the deaths. This reaches Harriet, Pell ("Carrow Street is the thing it most fears repeating"), Marisol ("believed for sixteen years… three nights too soon"), the rule, the Settled section, Pell's threat ("maintenance, or another Carrow Street"), scene 6 and the costs.

What B missed:
- Harriet's entry still says "Twenty years ago" while elsewhere it is now "sixteen years". The scene's "You were seventeen" doesn't fit "twenty years ago" either, since Marisol is now 35.
- The "nearest in kind" logic gets weaker. In rework 1 the light went to Harriet as "the adult most sure *it should have been me*", after the deaths. Now it goes to her before the deaths as "the adult most sure *I should be the one holding this girl up*". That is harder to believe of someone paid to keep the girl shallow.

### 4. The scene

**A ("What I'll Do")** reflects both reworks: Halsey Street nine days ago, June's cat keyring, and Noreen breaking the therapeutic frame to confess she lit up at six and locked. "It was like being a coat on a hook" ties Dani to the young Noreen. The payoff, where Dani changes to the chair facing the window, is quiet and earned. It's well made.

**B ("Marisol")** reflects both reworks: Harriet's apology for the two years, and Marisol's "Three nights… You kept me going too long, and I stopped too soon. We can split it if you like." The reveal comes through memory (the hands that went hot, "On the third night it had stopped. She had been relieved") and lands hard. Harriet's deliberate lie ("I don't know… the first lie she had told Marisol that she knew was a lie") is a strong, morally complicated turn that opens a new question. The texture is excellent: the gull and the bin, the bacon spitting.

Both are strong. **B's is slightly the better scene**, because it ends on a turn that makes things worse and pushes the story forward.

### 5. Has what it needs

**A: yes.** Every character wants something: Dani wants to keep fighting, Noreen wants Dani to be able to stop without a child paying, Bridget wants Noreen out of it, Marcus wants Dani in the field, and Teo wants to be like Dani. There are three tracked reader questions, and the stakes are concrete (Teo; another Halsey Street).

**B: yes.** Harriet wants a cure while hiding it, against Pell's paid terms. Dani wants Juno protected. Ines is the profession's conscience. Pell's secret is a ticking question. The stakes are a city undefended, an eleven-year-old sister, and Harriet's practice and body.

**Preference: B. Confidence: low.** Both are strong; A's reworks are cleaner, while B has more story to develop and the better scene.

## Magical girl therapist, pair 2

### 1. Keep developing

**A** (*The Maintenance Dose*: Ruth, Dani, June, the Clerk) states its rule well: "the brooch runs on a feeling the girl can't put down… So the town needs its girls to stay hurt." After the reworks it has a chain of strong reversals:
- Ruth reported June's dimming and deliberately relit her ("Dr. Ruth made me do Dad again. Third time. I'm bright as anything. I think I hate her.").
- Dani read that a year ago and asked for Ruth by name.
- The turn is "was it worth it?" Dani needs a yes, and Ruth can't say it.
- The Clerk ("speaks in clauses… never once told a girl the brooch was safe, only that it was necessary") is a memorable functionary.

**B** (Ines, Dee, Pip, Marcus, Ruth Abara) has a richer cast with real wants:
- Pip asks in confidence for Dee to be let go.
- Marcus grew up on the block Ruth saved.
- Dee's father calls carefully every Sunday.
- Ruth planned her sacrifice months ahead.

Its ending is bold: at half light Dee pulls Pip out instead of holding, Harrow Street is lost, and Marcus pulls the contract. It is also the only one of the four packets in this premise that doesn't end with the older woman lighting up.

I'd rather keep developing **A**, narrowly. Its central question, whether the trade was worth it, is sharper, and its reversals are cleaner. B has more material, but its structured format spreads attention across many threads.

### 2. Open questions

**A** settles the key points (June's death, the referral, the ending, what the story is about) and keeps two things open: "Whether Dani ever takes the full cure… leave the door open" and "What the Slack is. Better unexplained." Both are well chosen, but A shows no alternatives being weighed.

**B** lists only Decided answers, each tied to the characters' wants ("What does Dee choose, and at what cost? At half light Dee pulls Pip out instead of holding"). It doesn't weigh alternatives or keep anything open. After rework 2 one entry reads "Concerns Ines picks up", a template slip.

Neither packet shows candidates being weighed. A keeps its two open points with more purpose.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A is better.** A changes Ruth's guilt from curing June to reporting her and relighting her. This reaches:
- Ruth's want (the north bank lit, "because if it goes dark, what she did to June bought nothing")
- the Clerk's referral (to "the one therapist who has already proved she will relight a girl")
- scene 3, where Ruth now relights Dani ("what Dani last said to her sister… Dani goes white. The bank blazes all week")
- scene 4 (Ruth's report pinned to June's page)
- the diary entries
- the "was it worth it?" turn
- the brooch lighting "on the thing she stopped calling worth it"
- scene 8 (Dani fights like June did)
- the new terms ("nothing is reported")
- the Settled section

A missed very little: the reader's question "answer is on a ledger page" explains the referral only loosely.

B changes why Ines lost her light: she picked up her mother's call, and Ruth had arranged it. This reaches:
- Ines's rule ("never let a girl go back to who hurt her")
- Ruth's want ("Get Ines home")
- a new event (her mother starts calling)
- Fenwick's note
- the empty-chair scene ("words Ines never said to her mother")
- the file (her mother's number clipped to the intake sheet)
- a new ending beat (Ines dials)

That's good. What B missed: Dee's "stipend" stake is dropped without comment, and the Decided list is trimmed.

**Rework 2 (event moved in time): roughly even; A slightly better.** A moves Dani's discovery of the diary to a year before the story. This reaches:
- Dani's method ("go to the one therapist she knows will relight her, and never let on")
- the Clerk (now only "kept quiet")
- scene 1 ("wished to be in experienced hands", which Ruth mishears)
- scene 2 ("She watches Ruth's face throughout")
- scene 3 ("Ruth decides she never noticed")
- scene 4 (the request "in a fifteen-year-old's hand")
- scene 5, rewritten as "The order": Ruth tries a real cure, and Dani produces the diary and demands the dose
- scene 6 ("She needs a yes")
- the Settled section and the theme line ("or someone using you")

What A missed: Dani's at-stake line ("being only the girl whose sister died") isn't revisited, even though her motive is now a knowing bargain.

B moves Ines's lost light to Eastmarsh night itself ("Pick up, I've got it"). This reaches Ines's stake, Ruth's method, the timing of her mother's calls ("mostly on siren nights"), Fenwick's note (now "winter… next breach she'll send I. to the phone"), the file scene, the Sunday sirens, and a new Decided entry.

What B missed: the file still has Ines saying "I want to pick up" two sessions after Ruth's winter visit, yet she doesn't pick up until autumn. That gap isn't addressed.

### 4. The scene

**A ("The Confession")** reflects both reworks. Dani has left the diary open "the way you put down a hand of cards you've already won with." Ruth tells the part no diary could hold: "She never knew who asked for it." Dani's "Because I need it to be… I *asked* for it" lands, and the window cracks. Then: "Write it down… There's a box for it, isn't there." The launderette dryer knocking is a fine, controlled device. This is excellent.

**B ("Sunday")** reflects both reworks: the test sirens "as on Eastmarsh night", "Pick up, I've got it", and Fenwick's note showing Ruth "had decided". "Did Pip come here?" / "She did not say no" is handled well, and Dee's "Can I call you back… I will, actually" is a quiet, earned turn. There's a copy error (two speeches merged into one quotation), and the long backstory monologue is a bit expository.

Both are strong; **A's is the better scene.** Its turn comes from a conflict playing out in the room, not from telling.

### 5. Has what it needs

**A: yes.** Ruth wants the trade to have been worth it, Dani wants to stay lit (and, after rework 2, knowingly bargains for it), and the Clerk wants coverage. The reader's questions are tracked, and the stakes are a girl's life, the bank and Ruth's whole self-justification.

**B: yes.** It has more characters with real wants and active opposition (Pip against Marcus, Dee against her father, Ines against Ruth's legacy), four tracked questions, and concrete stakes (Harrow Street, Pip's next injury).

**Preference: A. Confidence: low.**
