## 6. Keep, change or drop

**Drop.** The tool did not help on the page, and the sessions' own logs show it pushing them to delete the story structure it was built to hold.

### The held-back premise

Both pairs went to notes at medium-high confidence, and in both the faults I found blind map onto what the tool session reports doing to fit the render.

**Pair 1** (tool is A). Blind, I marked A down for a Decided list that shows no alternatives, for a rework that deleted "Why does this work matter to Noreen?" and "Noreen books herself a therapist" without replacement, for an ending resolved administratively, and for Marcus never losing a scene. The log for that run says: the session weighed three placements for Halsey Street "in my head and left no record" because an open question would have printed and cost words; it merged two decided questions by deleting one; it deleted most present-day events; it deleted the Office as an actor; it removed Bridget's Bright going out as an origin of Noreen's drive and Teo's and Marcus's drives from scenes "they're really in"; it replaced ordering constraints with a made-up year. Each thing I found thin on the page is something the session cut because the tool charged for it.

**Pair 2** (tool is B). Blind, I called B's second rework "coherent but smaller" and its scene more expository, and noted it holds nothing open on purpose. The log says: "The model is richer than what fits; some true links were dropped only for length." Same-day ordering had to be faked with a fractional year. The mirror between the two phone calls, which is the whole point of the first rework, has no relation type and lives only in scene text. After the time move, constraints "were still satisfied but now said the wrong thing" and nothing flagged them. Free text saying "spring" and "months" was found by reading. The rework I judged smaller is the one where the session reports the tool offering no help and some hindrance.

### How much the tuned-on pairs moved me

Little, and in the same direction. I weigh them less as the rubric asks, but they are where the tool should have flattered itself, and it still lost both. The Thukker 1 tool run removed the one hook ("CONCORD is still out there"), deleted three settled questions with their rejected candidates, and reports that moving Mira's fusion left "three years dead", the ledger line, Ishu's stakes and the graveyard count all stale: "Nothing flagged them; I found them by reading." The Thukker 2 tool run deleted the event where Hask leaves the caravan, deleted answered questions, dropped a real relation (Haatakan's offer tempting Aino), and removed the ordering constraint the second rework depends on: "Nothing now guards that order." The verdict would be the same on the held-back pairs alone.

### What the tool was for, and whether it did it

The tool offers three services: a contradictions check, a way to find what touches a record, and a gaps report. Across the four logs:

- The contradictions check fired spuriously in all four runs, on events in the same year with an ordering constraint. It never fired on the real inconsistencies the reworks produced, because those were in free text.
- The touches query reaches records through actors but "can't say which summaries really depend on the changed fact, because summaries are free text."
- The gaps report flagged things the sessions judged correct as they stood (a broker's temperament, a want born of a condition, a dead character's wish) and missed every stale sentence.

So the job the tool exists for, keeping a story consistent under rework, was done by reading in both conditions. The notes sessions did it at least as well, and spent the words the tool sessions spent on link repetition on the things that won: weighed alternatives, a hook left open on purpose, an antagonist who loses a scene.

One comparison makes the point. The notes run on Thukker pair 1 wrote a scratch file listing three candidate reworks with a reason each and the one chosen. That is exactly the open-question-with-candidates structure the tool provides. The tool run on magical girl therapist pair 1 skipped the same kind of weighing because the tool would have printed it. The tool made the thing it models more expensive than doing it by hand.

### Why not change

Change is for a mixed result or visible promise. The result is not mixed. As for promise: the tool's wants, stakes and opposition fields did put clear wants on every tool page, but every notes page had them too, from the same craft advice. The fixes the logs ask for reach the core of the model: time units, causality, history, several missing relation types, and the render that drove the deletions. That is a redesign, not a patch. The next attempt should start from the gaps logs, not from this data model.

## 7. What was missing

The logs are consistent across all four runs, tuned-on and held-back alike, which itself says the tuning did not reach these.

**First, before any model change: the render cost.** Every log reports deleting true structure to meet the length target, because each declared link reprints a drive's want, each constraint prints in brackets, each decided question prints beside the facts it produced, and each open question prints all its candidates. This is upstream of most of what I found missing on the page. Until declared structure stops counting against the words, any type added below will be deleted for the same reason. Render links, constraints, decided questions and rejected candidates compactly or on demand, and keep them out of the word count.

Then, in the model, in the order the packets suffered:

1. **A chain of causes between events.** All four logs. Positions carry order, not direction. Moving Aino's desertion turned an effect into a cause and "the timeline check had nothing to object to." Fenn being dropped because of Celeste became "two numbers that happen to agree." A call and a breach on one night meant "during, and causing part of" and could only be marked simultaneous. Without cause links, a time move cannot be checked for meaning, and a time move is one of the two reworks.
2. **A way to reach what text only mentions.** All four logs. Each move and each motive change left summaries, stakes and world facts quoting the old timing or the old motive, and the tool could not see them. A move should list records whose text mentions the moved event or its date. The cheapest form, suggested in one log, is letting a summary refer to an event's position instead of restating it.
3. **History.** All four logs. A reworked answer leaves the old chosen answer as a rejected candidate with no mark that it was once true; sessions then deleted the question for length, so "the history lives only in git." A superseded mark and an archived state for decided questions that stay in the file but out of the render.
4. **Types the model lacks**, each named by at least one log: plant and payoff; an echo or mirror between two characters or two events; a reveal that revises what a past event meant; a drive outcome, including a want given up rather than met; an intended ambiguity, distinct from an open question awaiting an answer; an event that is only what a scene shows; same-day or within-year ordering; a drive that comes from a condition rather than an event; an actor present in scenes without a drive of its own.
5. **Different gap reports.** Stop reporting equal position plus an ordering constraint as a contradiction. Stop demanding an origin for a temperament. After a move, flag constraints whose gap has gone to zero or near it, and flag free text that mentions the old date.

If I could change one thing first it would be the render cost. If I could change one thing in the model first it would be a cause link between events.

## 8. Doubts

- **Format giveaway.** I noticed in Part 1 that one packet in each pair was a structured list and the other prose, and said so. My preference could be tracking how pleasant prose is to read rather than story quality. Against that: every fault I named was a story fault (a hook removed, alternatives not weighed, an antagonist who reads a document and leaves), and the logs independently confirm those things were cut for length. I cannot rule the bias out.
- **Sample.** Four pairs, one run per cell, one judge reading one render. The Thukker pairs were medium confidence. A different session on either side could have moved a pair.
- **The length target is a condition, not the tool.** A target of about 1,500 words hits the tool harder than notes because its render repeats itself. At a larger budget some of the deletions would not have happened. But the tool at this budget is what was tested, and the budget is the same one notes met with room for weighed alternatives.
- **The render is not the model.** The logs say the underlying file held more than what was rendered. A judge of the data file might find structure I did not see. The rubric judges the page, and the page is what a writer would draft from.
- **Effort.** Tool runs took longer at the sketch step, which suggests time went into working around the tool. Not graded, but consistent with the logs.
