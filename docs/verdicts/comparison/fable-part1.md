# Part 1: blind judgments

Judged on the page only. I noticed that within each pair one packet is a prose sketch and the other a structured list (people, events, scenes, decided), but I have not let that steer the calls below. Where the structured format hurt or helped the *story*, I say so in story terms.

---

## Thukker, pair 1 (A: "Lot Nine" / Tavik, Kaari, Hesk, the Auditor; B: Tavik, Ishu, Drekka, the Archivist)

### 1. Keep developing

**A, by a clear margin.** After the reworks, A's Tavik wants something that runs head-on into his partner's want: he goes to the yard "to become what took her sister," and doesn't tell Kaari. That gives the midpoint a real reversal (he takes Tenna's side; "his envy cracks, since being finished came with a governor") and turns both Act IV offers into temptations instead of threats. The order *Keep him out of hulls* and Hesk dragging him out by the sockets at nineteen come round again at the climax with her hand on the same sockets, not pulling. Tenna's "I want to be unleashed, not undone" is a line I'd wait a whole story for. The ending (he plugs into the *Dust Owed* for an hour and comes back; Tenna feels another ship-shaped mind) leaves the right thing open.

B has strong material too: Rask's death as the thing Tavik carries, the knot-song counting as a concrete device for "holding a hull without becoming it," the graveyard of half-minds, "a ship longer than she was a girl." But its antagonist and conspiracy are thinner. The buyer "inside CONCORD" is a label, not a person; Haruka's end ("survives, disowned by her buyer") is a shrug; the climax in scene 11 stacks five things (second hull, Drekka shaming the elders, a Jovian ship flickering past, guns on the yard, Mira won't fire on their mother) without one of them being the turn. Ishu is thin: after rework 2 she is "the girl at an empty grave," but nothing in the scenes gives her a decision as sharp as Kaari's "refuses for both" in A.

### 2. Open questions

A settles its big questions with reasons visible on the page ("There is no birth family to find. That's the point."), and holds one genuinely open as ambiguity rather than vagueness: the Auditor "kept him unfinished as a mercy or as a control. He doesn't know which, either." The ending is marked *left open on purpose* and the open thing (another ship-mind out there) is a real hook, not a dodge. A doesn't show alternatives weighed against each other, but what's open is held with intent.

B's "Decided" list is thorough and some answers do work for the story ("Rask's death stays his" is a good call that protects Tavik's guilt). But nothing is left open deliberately; the one hook ("CONCORD is still out there") is removed in rework 1 and not replaced. Rework 1 also deletes the decision about whether Mira caused the accident without saying why, which loses a useful settled point rather than reopening it.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A better.** A's change (Tavik wants to be *finished*) reaches everything it should: the "what it's about" line, the thread sentence ("nobody can finish him either"), the Act I escape ("He wants to know what it's for; she never wants to see one again"), the midpoint, both offers, the climax ("half hoping it will"), the pull-out, and the coda. The hauler he "has never let himself plug into" is a new detail that pays off in the last line. I found no leftover that contradicts the new motive.

B's change (leave to have the socket cut out) also reaches the scenes it needs: Drekka now asks him to stay, the Archivist "could remove the socket, and won't," scene 5 makes him use the thing he came to be rid of, and the ending has him refuse the cure. Two things it missed. The original ending had Tavik "fly the empty Titan unfused"; that second hull now has no stated fate. And the Hallak caravan is listed as "Allied with Tavik" while the elders are still the people who "refuse to ride for him," which reads oddly even if the logic (both want him safe) is there.

**Rework 2 (time): roughly even, slight edge to A for payoff.** A moves Hesk's confession to the final battle and makes it structural: in Act III the caravan sends him off, Dass's logs half-answer the question, in Act IV the caravan arrives uninvited under the Auditor's last order, and the climax image (hand on the sockets, doesn't pull) is the moved event. The thread sentence and the ending both note the caravan's pay is gone with the relay. Nothing stale was left.

B moves Mira's fusion nine years earlier and tracks the arithmetic carefully: payments "outlive Mira's funeral by eleven years," the graveyard is "one a year since Mira" and still twelve wrecks, Ishu was "eight at Mira's funeral," Haruka's stake becomes "murder," and the mother's offer becomes "the sister she buried, grown into a body." That is more consequence followed through than A's move required. One loose end: "Mira joins her mother's subsidiary" is gone, but how a fourteen-year-old's fusion became Lattice's "first Titan mind" when Tavik was a first-batch child ten years earlier is not touched.

### 4. The scene

Both scenes reflect their reworked stories exactly. A's "The Hand" is the climax from Hesk's point of view, and it dramatizes both reworks at once: the want to be finished ("Not pain. Joy."), the order, the earlier pull, and her choosing not to. The Auditor's "I have not been sure, for some years, what I mean by it" is the sketch's open question spoken aloud. The ending exchange ("She'd have let me stay." / "I know." / "I came out anyway." / "I know. I saw.") lands the whole story in four lines.

B's "The graveyard of half-minds" is also very good: the counting-child wreck, the smell of oranges, Mira's "I had fourteen of them. I've had twelve of the other kind," and Mira finishing the knot-song ("*we carry home*") when his voice sticks. It reflects rework 1 (he came to be rid of the socket; the Archivist's "I could") and rework 2 (one wreck a year since her). It has two stray typos ("fourteen kilometres long,." and "like his own arm,.").

**A's is the better scene**, because the point-of-view character makes the decisive choice under a clock, and because it is the moved event itself. B's is a strong discovery scene.

### 5. Has what it needs

Both do. A: Tavik wants to be finished; Kaari wants Tenna home as she was; Tenna wants to be unleashed; Asami and the Auditor each hold a real offer; stakes are a Navy sale and a governor. B: Tavik wants the socket out; Ishu wants Mira home; Drekka wants him home; the elders want him away; Haruka wants her Titans delivered; stakes are a second hull and Rask's ghost. B's Velan and the CONCORD buyer are the weakest wants on either page.

**Preference: A. Confidence: medium.**

## Thukker, pair 2 (A: Sev, Saisi, Kaeli, Haldra, Kaaven, Oveg; B: Hask, Aino, Kiri, Orsa, Haatakan, Ondrai)

### 1. Keep developing

**A.** Its antagonist is the best on either page: Kaaven, ex-Navy, who watched a capsuleer Titan warp out and wants "a Titan that cannot leave," and whose fleet abandons him, "the thing he feared most." Oveg gets a real answer to carry out ("humans did what Jove did, but asked first"). Saisi's arc ends on "love that means not getting what you want," and after the reworks Sev's hatred of lies runs straight into her talent for them and into her father's deal over Kaeli ("She asks him who he is really angry at"). The second rework makes Sev buy a fleet, "doing what Haldra did, and Saisi says so," and then has the bought crews scatter while the unpaid caravan arrives. That is a story whose parts argue with each other.

B has one idea I'd steal: the core is keyed to Hask, Kiri is burning out in it, and "the armada is the cure," a linked fleet with Hask as hub, which is a caravan. That gives B a clock A lacks (months left, lost words). But B's climax doesn't land: Hask "sends Haatakan the ledger; the director withdraws" because "he won't be anyone's test animal." The antagonist reads a document and leaves. Ondrai is courteous and thin, and his exit ("asks Hask to come with him, and leaves alone") is a line rather than a scene. Aino's and Kiri's swap is good, but how Aino comes to be at the broker's vault looking for a sister she doesn't yet know is in a Titan is never put on the page.

### 2. Open questions

A keeps a "Still open" list with real candidates and the cost of each: Kaaven lives (sequel hook) or dies ("through his crew's refusal, not the heroes'"); Sev and Saisi romantic or "siblings-in-arms," leaning unspoken; Lusse "probably on her feet." After rework 2 it adds a new worry it knows it must solve: "Whether Haldra's arrival reads as rescue by chance. It must not: she paid for it." That is the rubric's ideal: alternatives weighed against the rest of the story.

B's "Decided" answers are serviceable but it holds nothing open on purpose, and rework 1 prunes three decisions (Did Kiri choose; What becomes of Ondrai; Hask's background) rather than reopening or replacing them. The gap about why Aino is at the broker at all is left vague in all three versions.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A better, though both are thorough.** A's change (Sev leaves in anger over the Jovian payments) reaches Oveg ("paid a Thukker hauler pilot to raise him and report on him"), Lusse ("found the Jovian payments"), the turn ("Oveg paid for a specimen. The Thukker took the money and made Sev a person anyway"), scenes 1, 3, 4, 7, 8, the climax ("whole, and belonging to nobody. It is the thing he left to find") and the ending. "The caravan *says* it found him" is a nice small ripple. No contradictions left.

B's change (Hask sold the core himself) is well traced: "pays for everything," "waits to be thanked," "He says he's a buyer, not the seller," "owing, nearly agrees," scene 7 retitled "What he owes," "carrying Kiri as kin, not debt." What it missed: the event "Hask leaves the caravan" is deleted and his departure is now only implied inside scene 2; and Orsa is still listed as *opposing* Hask's want to buy the core back, which needs a sentence it doesn't get.

**Rework 2 (time): A better.** A moves the homecoming to the climax and does the hard part: scene 7 becomes "Not going home," Oveg's confession now turns anger to shame without sending him home ("*Raised:* will he ever face her?"), Lusse sells the position to "one other buyer," the paid crews scatter, and the pull back from becoming the Titan is "feeling Haldra's hauler under fire." The ending becomes "paid for twice, and kept." One small loss: "Her loyalty to her family breaks and is partly mended" was cut from Rikkan's scene for no stated reason.

B moves Aino's escape before Kiri's fusion so that "Aino's freedom is what put her sister in the Titan." It reaches Kiri's want ("hide what Aino's escape cost"), the events, scene 3 ("where Aino has stolen for a living since she ran"), and scene 9 ("Aino, who thought she left owing nothing"). It is coherent but smaller, and it leaves the original fuzziness about why Aino is at the vault in place, now with "Kiri's letters have stopped" doing all the work.

### 4. The scene

A's "The Cradle" is the climax from inside the leash. It reflects both reworks precisely: the bought crews jump ("they took the money and they jumped"), the caravan drops out of warp unasked, and what pulls him back is the Mammoth "with a cracked forward plate he would have known blind" and Haldra's voice ("*we are not turning round!*"). Kaeli's "Are you here to hold my leash, or to take my place?" / "*Neither.*" / "*Then do it properly*" is a clean turn.

B's "The voice in the hull" is a good discovery scene: Kiri losing Aino's name and finding it when Hask steps aboard, "Little fish," Hask looking "like someone waiting for a bill," and the closing realization that "the man who'd sold the box was now the only thing keeping her sister whole." It reflects rework 1 (he sold it, two years trying to buy it back) and rework 2 (she ran from a contract and "nobody had come after her," which she took as owing nothing). Kiri's lie ("They paid. Very well.") is exactly her want in action.

**A's is the better scene**: higher stakes, a decision made on the page, and it *is* the moved event. B's is quieter and well-observed.

### 5. Has what it needs

A: yes on every count; Kaaven and Oveg both want things that matter, and the open list is honest about what's not yet earned. B: Hask, Aino, Kiri and Orsa all want things and oppose each other well; Kiri's burn-out is a strong stake. Haatakan and Ondrai are wants without pressure, and the question "who is behind the project" is answered by a ledger rather than a scene.

**Preference: A. Confidence: medium.**

## Magical girl therapist, pair 1 (A: Noreen, Bridget, Dani, Teo, Marcus; B: Harriet, Dani, Juno, Pell, Ines, Marisol)

### 1. Keep developing

**B.** Both stories arrive at the same image, a 54-year-old therapist taking up the power so a child doesn't have to, but B surrounds it with more that can go wrong. Pell is a real character ("smells of struck matches," "feet not reaching the floor," pays in old coins, "has no rule for what it is seeing"). Ines the supervisor is the profession's conscience in the room ("You're not treating Dani. You're treating Marisol."). Marisol is a living consequence who, after the reworks, has carried the wrong guilt for sixteen years. Juno has been following her sister at night and Pell "looks at Juno a moment too long." The coda ("It was *mine*." / "I know.") refuses the clean ending. B's "Costs, so the ending isn't clean" section is the sort of thinking the premise needs.

A is tight and honest: the Bright goes "to whoever steps first," adults "stop to think," Teo practises the stance in the hallway mirror, Bridget "makes tea standing up." Rework 2 puts Halsey Street two weeks into Noreen's work, which is a smart move. But its end ("Terms": hazard pay, adult recruitment, under-18s stood down) resolves the premise administratively, Marcus never gets a scene where he loses, and the one question still open at the finish (Bridget in the back row) is quieter than B's.

### 2. Open questions

B is the best packet of the eight on this question. Its sketch *shows the weighing*: the Carrow Street answer "brings back the warm hands, gives 'nearest in kind' a second meaning, and makes Harriet's guilt specific. A former magical girl, or Pell letting the deaths happen, would both do less work." That is two candidates rejected for stated reasons. It also keeps a "Still open (fine to leave for drafting)" list with a leaning ("light that feels like a held hand, not beams"), and marks what stays open on purpose (Juno).

A's "Decided" list settles things cleanly and the answers are good (the Bright "answers to whoever steps first"), but no alternatives are shown, nothing is marked open on purpose, and rework 1 deletes "Noreen books herself a therapist" and "Why does this work matter to Noreen?" without replacement.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): both thorough; B's change is deeper and reaches further.** A's change (Noreen lit up at six and froze; Bridget took it) reaches Bridget's want and line in the kitchen ("You lit up first. Then you locked, and I paid six years."), scene 2 ("Noreen puts her pen down"), the confession in scene 5, "has done her freezing in advance... and she holds," Bridget's "she held it well," and the Decided answer. The table is kept as where Noreen "finishes the night." Nothing contradicts. Small miss: the "Why does this work matter to Noreen?" decision is deleted rather than rewritten.

B's change (Harriet kept Marisol lit rather than curing her; now she means to cure Dani in secret) reaches the shape line, Harriet's want and flaw ("she wants the cure for herself"), Pell's reason for hiring her, Ines's questions, Marisol ("isn't cured; came apart"), the rule ("heals, *or breaks*"), the central contradiction ("her old mistake turned round"), scenes 2 through 8, the costs, and the questions (new: "Will Pell find out?"). It removed "Will Dani let go?" from the reader's list although scene 8 still turns on it; that is a bookkeeping gap, not a contradiction. One arithmetic wobble introduced here: "Twenty years ago Pell asked her" to treat a girl who broke at nineteen and is now thirty-five, which is sixteen years ago plus two years of sessions.

**Rework 2 (time): both followed through; B's does more work.** A moves Halsey Street to two weeks in. It reaches Dani's stake ("June was next"), Marcus (now counts "the city's Wardens," not four), the event order (Fenn dropped after Celeste; Dani's earlier panic now covered by "Sade, sixteen," since June wasn't a Warden yet, a good catch), the kitchen ("The news is on"), the deadline scene, scene 5 ("hasn't slept since Halsey Street"), and "June first" in the terms. Clean.

B moves the light's visit to Harriet to three nights *before* Carrow Street, so "the thirty-one deaths come from Harriet's refusal." It reaches Harriet's bio, Pell's fear ("Carrow Street is the thing it most fears repeating"), Marisol's own guilt ("three nights too soon"), the rule, Q1, scenes 3, 4, 6, 7, 8 ("on the street where she once didn't turn up") and the costs ("Marisol has to be told whose fault they were"). The one thing not quite reconciled: Pell "lost the light for a winter" survives from the previous version while the settled section now says it guttered on the third night; both can be true but the sketch doesn't say so.

### 4. The scene

A's "What I'll Do" reflects the reworked story: nine days after Halsey, June "in Year Seven" with a cat keyring, Noreen's confession ("I was six... I lit up. And then I locked."), the Thursday-at-six offer, and Dani moving to the chair facing the window. It is a solid session scene. Its weakness is that Noreen explains the rule and her history in longish speeches; Dani's "Can I just," and not finishing, is the best beat.

B's "Marisol" reflects both reworks and adds a choice the sketch didn't promise: Marisol hands Harriet "somebody else's" debt ("We can split it if you like"), Harriet remembers the three nights, and then *lies* ("I don't know"), which the costs section had already prepared for. "Don't do it to the new one." / "I'm trying not to." / "Try harder." Two adults, a reversal, a lie that sets up the next scene.

**B's is the better scene.**

### 5. Has what it needs

Both have it. A: Dani wants the signature; Noreen wants Dani out without a child paying; Marcus wants her in the field; Bridget wants Noreen out of it; Teo wants to be Dani; the stake is Teo. B: the same shape plus Pell with its own rules, Ines asking who the client is, and Marisol as the evidence. B's "central contradiction" paragraph states the engine in three sentences.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium-high.**

## Magical girl therapist, pair 2 (A: "The Maintenance Dose" / Ruth, Dani Szabo, the Clerk, June; B: Ines, Dee, Pip, Marcus, Ruth Abara, Fenwick)

### 1. Keep developing

**A.** The rule is stated in one line that is also the story's accusation: "the brooch runs on a feeling the girl can't put down... So the town needs its girls to stay hurt." The Clerk is a fine invention (cat-sized, moth-winged, "has never told a girl the brooch was safe, only that it was necessary," keeps a ledger). After the reworks the engine is as tight as any here: Ruth reported June and relit her on the Clerk's instruction; Dani read the diary a year ago and *asked for Ruth by name* because she wants the relighting; the turn is "was it worth it?" and Ruth can't say yes. Rework 2 turns the referral mystery inside out ("I did not choose you, Dr. Akande-Price. She did.") and the ending ("brighter, not better"; Ruth "pays the rest herself"; "Not yet") is earned.

B is a quieter, truer-to-life version and I'd read it: Dee's father ringing "at twenty past, like he's got an alarm set so he doesn't seem keen," the empty chair, Pip asking in confidence, the file at the front of the drawer, "Did Pip come here?" answered by not saying no, and an ending that refuses rescue (Harrow Street is lost; Marcus pulls the contract; Dee calls her father back). But Ines's climax is watched from a shelter roof, Marcus and Pip are functions, and giving "Wants" to two dead characters (Fenwick, Ruth Abara) shows the seams. B's story depends on the reader accepting "a girl's power runs on the wound... no one has found a way around it" and then doesn't make the Office or its liaison push back hard enough to test it.

### 2. Open questions

A settles with reasons and keeps the right things open: "Whether Dani ever takes the full cure. Leave the door open." and "What the Slack is. Better unexplained." Its settled list states what the story is *about* and changes that line in rework 2 to add "or someone using you," which shows the session re-weighing the theme against the new structure. The reader-questions list is reworked each time to say where the answer now lands ("In scene 4 the answer is that it didn't").

B's Decided list is specific ("At half light Dee pulls Pip out instead of holding") and rework 2 adds a new decision rather than deleting. But nothing is held open deliberately, and the "Will Dee cut her father off?" answer ("neither cuts him off nor heals out") is stated as a decision without the alternatives being weighed on the page.

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (motivation): A better.** A's change (Ruth reported June's dimming and relit her over three sessions) reaches Ruth's self-justification ("the alternative is that she did something unforgivable for nothing"), the three-way bind, the Clerk's leverage ("Ruth's report is in its ledger, in her own handwriting"), scene 3 (Ruth relights Dani: "what the last thing Dani said to her sister was"), scene 4 (Ruth asks to hand Dani off), the diary's second entry ("I think I hate her"), the confession ("was it worth it?"), the brooch lighting "on the thing she stopped calling worth it," and the terms ("nothing is reported"). No leftovers contradict.

B's change (Ines lost her light by picking up her mother's call) is also carefully traced: Ines "hasn't spoken to her mother since Eastmarsh," Ruth Abara's want becomes "Get Ines home," Fenwick's note is rewritten, scene 5's file now says "I want to pick up" with the mother's number clipped, scene 4 adds "words Ines never said to her mother," and the ending adds "Ines dials the number from her file." It removes "the stipend stops" from Dee's stakes and "Her girls stay certified longest" from Ines's method for no stated reason; scene 1 still relies on the latter.

**Rework 2 (time): A better, and the better of the two moves.** A moves Dani's discovery of the diary a year before the story. It reaches Dani's method ("go to the one therapist she knows will relight her, and never let on"), the Clerk's role ("saw no reason to refuse, or to tell Ruth who had asked"), scene 1 ("wished to be in experienced hands"; "Ruth hears it as the Clerk's own choice"), scene 2 ("She watches Ruth's face throughout"), scene 3 ("Ruth decides she never noticed"), scene 4 (the request in a fifteen-year-old's hand), a new scene 5 ("The order": Ruth finally treats her properly; Dani produces the diary and says "That's what you're for"), scene 6 ("She needs a yes, because she has bet her own life on the same trade"), and the Settled list. The cost is that the Clerk loses agency; the session accepts that and says so ("only a functionary who kept quiet").

B moves Ines's pick-up to Eastmarsh night at Ruth's word ("Pick up, I've got it"). It reaches the events, Ruth's method, Fenwick's note ("next breach she'll send I. to the phone and hold alone"), scene 5 ("Ruth chose that night months before"), scene 6 ("Sirens testing, as on Eastmarsh night"), and a new decision ("Ines thinks Ruth spoke on impulse, and blames herself"). It is coherent and the echo with Dee's Sunday call is real, but it changes less of the story than A's move does.

### 4. The scene

A's "The Confession" reflects the twice-reworked story exactly: the diary "face up, the way you put down a hand of cards you've already won with"; "Do Dad. Or do June, I'm not fussy"; Ruth's report ("I could have left the box empty. Nobody checks the box"); "So was it worth it?" / "Because I need it to be... I *asked* for it"; "I can't"; the brooch going off and the window cracking; "Write it down. If I end up on the kerb tonight. There's a box for it, isn't there." The dryer knocking is a good clock. It ends with her hand on the drawer, which is scene 7's doorstep.

B's "Sunday" reflects its reworks too: the mother ringing on siren nights, "Pick up, I've got it," Ruth having "decided... months before," "Did Pip come here?" answered by silence, "Can I call you back... I will, actually," and the drawer "Not yet." It is a careful, humane scene. It is also more expository (Ines tells her history in one block), and it has a formatting slip (two of Ines's speeches run together in one paragraph, and a second "Ines said" tag on a paragraph already hers).

**A's is the better scene.**

### 5. Has what it needs

A: Ruth wants the bank lit so June's death "bought" something; Dani wants to stay lit and, after rework 2, to be relit by the person she hates; the Clerk wants coverage; stakes are the north bank and a fifteen-year-old going out "like someone who wants to be hurt." Every want opposes another. B: Dee wants her light back; Pip wants her out alive; Marcus wants the ward lit; Ines wants her girls lit and is the one who has to change. Stakes are Harrow Street and Pip's neck. B's Marcus and the father are thin, and B's antagonism is mostly inside Ines, which the structure handles less well than A's three-way bind.

**Preference: A. Confidence: medium-high.**
