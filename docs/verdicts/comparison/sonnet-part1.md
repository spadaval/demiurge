## Magical girl therapist, pair 1
A = Noreen / Dani / Bridget / Marcus (Wardens, Squalls, the Bright). B = Harriet / Dani / Pell / Ines / Marisol (the light, Hollows, Carrow Street).

**1. Keep developing.** B, narrowly.
- B has a bigger central pressure. Healing the girl disarms the city and may hand the fight to her 11-year-old sister, and the therapist's training forbids the only decent way out, which is stepping in herself.
- B also has a hidden twist that recasts earlier beats: the light came to Harriet. The warm hands in scene 1 are planted for it, and Ines and Pell each push on a different part of her.
- B has a costs section ("so the ending isn't clean").
- A is also well built. Noreen wants Dani out, but a therapist doesn't choose for a client. Bridget opposes her. The stairwell pays off "she did her calculating in advance".
- A's ending is tidier. Noreen steps in, Bridget softens, and "properly asleep" is the closing note. Its supporting cast (Marcus, Teo) is thinner on opposition after Marcus's early scenes.

**2. Open questions.**
- A: the "Decided" section settles every question: what the Bright answers to, what happens when Dani can't, what happened under the table. Several are good answers, but nothing is held open. After rework 1 the under-the-table answer becomes "Noreen has always known and never said", a nice nuance. Overall it is flattened early.
- B: weighs the Carrow Street answer against alternatives in the text ("a former magical girl, or Pell letting the deaths happen, would both do less work"). That is a real use of candidates. It leaves two small things open (Ines, the look of the power) and keeps Juno/Pell's last look open on purpose. Better than A, though the main mystery is still settled in the sketch.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1 (core motivation): B followed it through more thoroughly. B flipped Harriet from "cured Marisol" to "kept Marisol lit". That reached her want and flaw, the central contradiction, the Settled section, the Ines/Pell lines, Marisol's character, scenes 1–8 and the waiting-list. A changed Noreen from "put under the table" to "froze, and Bridget paid". That also reached Bridget's want, the stairwell, the kitchen scene, scene 5 and Decided. It dropped the "books herself a therapist" beat and the "Why does this work matter" answer without replacement, which loses a little.
- Rework 2 (event moved in time): A is the more visible, structural move. A pulled Halsey Street into the story, two weeks in, so the cost happens on the page. It reordered events, added news in the kitchen, changed Marcus's stake, and changed June to Sade in the earlier panic attack to avoid a clash. Small misses: the "she wants it to stop happening in fights" line was deleted from scene 1, and the Office group entry was dropped. B moved the light-passing to three nights before Carrow Street. That reached Marisol, Pell's threat ("another Carrow Street"), scenes 3, 4, 6 and 7, and the Settled section. A miss in B: "twenty years ago" is still used in Harriet's entry while the scene says sixteen. A slight clash is left in the timeline (two years' work, age 19). Close; I'd give rework 2 to A slightly and rework 1 to B.

**4. The scene.**
- A (Noreen tells Dani): reflects both reworks clearly (Halsey Street nine days ago, June, "I got there first … then I locked", "I'll catch it this time"). It is moving. It is also heavily expository, with long speeches, and the confession lands as a speech.
- B (Marisol): reflects both reworks (apology after sixteen years, "three nights", the hot hands remembered, Marisol carrying the guilt). It has a real turn: Harriet comes to apologise and is handed Marisol's guilt instead. It ends on a quiet, hard beat, the lie "I don't know". The better scene is B.

**5. Has what it needs.**
- A: yes. Wants, opposition, stakes and three reader questions are all clear.
- B: yes. It has the paid-to-keep-her-sick pressure, a secret, Pell's threat, and Dani's fear for Juno.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium.**

## Magical girl therapist, pair 2
A = Ruth / Dani / the Clerk (Calder Falls, brooch runs on a feeling the girl can't put down). B = Ines / Dee / Pip / Marcus / Ruth Abara (Hollows, power runs on a wound).

**1. Keep developing.** A.
- The world rule is the engine. "The town needs its girls to stay hurt" makes the therapy itself the moral problem.
- Ruth wants a forgiveness only Dani can give, and Dani wants a signature. The Clerk is an active schemer. Three reader questions are raised and paid in distinct scenes. The ending is non-clean: "brighter, not better", no absolution.
- B has a good ensemble and one very good beat, Pip's confidence that Ines cannot honour or deny. The Harrow Street climax, with Dee pulling Pip out rather than holding, is a good choice. But B's engine is more mechanical: the file, the call, and the chain of who-told-whom.

**2. Open questions.**
- A: held open in a useful way. Whether Dani takes the cure is "leave the door open". What the Slack is stays unexplained. After rework 2 it also leaves open whether Haldra's arrival reads as chance (it must not). Most mysteries are settled early, but the settled answers are strong and weighed.
- B: the "Decided" list answers everything early, including who Ondrai is and why the power faded. The sketch has little left open. Rework 1 deleted the "Hask-background" style answers, so it sometimes leaves things vague rather than weighed.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1: A, more thoroughly. June's death changed from Ruth's cure and silence to Ruth's obedience: she reported the dimming and relit June. That reached Ruth's want and history, the Clerk's method, "If Ruth lets Dani heal", the waiting-list, scenes 1–9 and the Settled section. B changed why Ines's power faded (a mother's call, not therapy). That reached Ruth Abara's want, the file note, scenes 4, 5, 6 and 8, the ending (Ines dials her mother), and Dee's father. B is solid but touches fewer scenes.
- Rework 2: A moved Dani's discovery of the diary a year earlier, making the referral Dani's request. It reached the Clerk, the "Wants" section, the waiting-list, scenes 1–6, 9, and the "Settled" section. B moved Ines's pick-up to Eastmarsh night, which reached the file note, Ruth's plan since winter, and scenes 5 and 6. A left behind nothing much. B has a small leftover in the ordering/labels ("Ines's power fades" removed but "Why did Ines's power fade at 17?" still stands). A is better on both.

**4. The scene.**
- A ("The Confession"): reflects both reworks. Dani has known a year, asks "was it worth it?" and needs a yes. Ruth can't say it, the window cracks, and the bottom drawer is left unopened. Tight, with a clear turn and strong callbacks ("Is there a box for it").
- B ("Sunday"): reflects both reworks (sirens, "Pick up, I've got it", Ruth's plan). The Pip question works. But Ines gives a long expository speech, and there is a visible formatting glitch in the dialogue ("…got brighter." "I had a teammate…"). The better scene is A.

**5. Has what it needs.**
- A: yes. Ruth, Dani and the Clerk all want things, with real opposition and stakes (a dark bank, a lost client).
- B: yes. It has plenty of wants, opposition and stakes, and arguably too many threads.

**Preference: A. Confidence: medium.**

## Thukker, pair 1
A = Tavik / Kaari / Tenna / Hesk / Asami / the Auditor (Lot Nine, the relay). B = Tavik / Ishu / Mira / Drekka / Haruka / the Archivist (Lattice, the knots).

**1. Keep developing.** A, clearly.
- It reads as a complete story: caravan, heist comedy, midpoint reversal (Tenna doesn't want rescuing), armada-building where each recruit uncovers a conspiracy, then a climax with two offers.
- After rework 1 the two leads want opposite things (he wants to be finished, she wants to undo what was done to her sister). That is a real collision. Hesk's love-as-keeping and the Auditor's "mercy or control" reflect the theme.
- B has good textures (knots with counting-songs, the graveyard of half-minds), but Tavik's want ("master what he is") is vague. It also has many scenes with thin opposition. Drekka opposes the elders more than Tavik.

**2. Open questions.**
- A: settles a lot early (what Tavik is, why he left). But it keeps the right thing open: the Auditor does not know whether his act was mercy or control, and the ending is "left open on purpose".
- B: the Decided list answers nearly everything, including whether Mira caused the accident ("No"). It reads settled rather than weighed. The best open item (the Archivist who could and won't) is useful.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1: A changed why Tavik leaves (from "Hesk sold me" to "I want to be finished; the order was Keep him out of hulls"). That reached the theme line, wants and fears, the midpoint, the offers, the climax (he chooses to come out), the ending (plugs into the Dust Owed), and the reader questions. Very thorough. B changed Tavik's goal to having the socket cut out. That reached the caravan's alliance with him, the Archivist's choice, scenes 2, 5, 7, 8 and 11, and the ending (he keeps it). But scene 8's "without his socket, only Mira could" is confusing, since he still has it. B is a notch behind.
- Rework 2: A delayed Hesk's confession to the climax, so the nineteen-year-old grab is echoed (she doesn't pull). It reached the Questions, the Ending and the Settled section, and it was handled with only minor residue. B moved Mira's fusion to age fourteen, twelve years ago. That reached Ishu (eight at the funeral), Haruka's stakes, the graveyard ("one a year") and Mira's refusal. One inconsistency is not commented on: the Lattice hauler with a fused-socket infant at year 0 predates Mira, "Lattice's first Titan mind". A is better on both.

**4. The scene.**
- A ("The Hand"): Hesk with her hand on his sockets, choosing not to pull. It reflects both reworks directly (the order, the confession an hour earlier, the nineteen-year-old grab, the Auditor's "I have not been sure what I mean by it"). Controlled and emotionally earned.
- B ("The graveyard"): reflects both reworks (twelve, one a year, "longer than I was a girl", the Archivist's "I could"). It has good moments (the counting song, Mira taking the other end). But it has typos (",." twice) and confusing lines ("I had fourteen of them … twelve of the other kind"). The better scene is A.

**5. Has what it needs.** Both do. A is stronger: a real antagonist pair (Asami and Kaaven-like pressure, the Auditor's offer), clear stakes for Tenna, and reader questions with payoffs. B's stakes are clear but spread out.

**Preference: A. Confidence: high.**

## Thukker pair 2
A = Sev / Saisi / Kaeli / Haldra / Kaaven / Oveg / Lusse. B = Hask / Aino / Kiri / Orsa / Haatakan / Ondrai.

**1. Keep developing.** A, by a small margin.
- A has the clearer spine. Saisi wants her sister back as she was, and Kaeli wants never to go back. That collision is the emotional core, with Kaaven as a driven antagonist (he wants a Titan that cannot leave), a betrayer in Lusse, and a climax that turns on Sev choosing.
- B has the more elegant theme. Hask's sale of the core and Aino's escape both put Kiri in the Titan, and the story is about nobody having to pay for a place. The "armada is the cure" resolution is inventive. B also has more threads (Ondrai, the swap, Saevel's message), several revealed late. It risks being busy, and Haatakan is the thinner antagonist.

**2. Open questions.**
- A: leaves real candidates open (Sev and Saisi romantic or not, whether Kaaven survives, Lusse's fate), and the rework adds "Haldra's arrival must not read as chance". The big answers (what Sev is, who Oveg is) are settled, but with weight.
- B: the Decided list answers most things early. In rework 1 some answers were removed (Hask's background, Ondrai's fate) and not replaced, so they go vague rather than weighed. The waiting-list still asks "What is Hask?", which is answered in scene 8.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1: A changed Sev's leaving from love to anger at a bought belonging (Haldra's ledger). It reached Oveg (paid Haldra to raise and report), Lusse, Saisi's lies, Sev's reaction to Rikkan, the homecoming, the climax ("belonging to nobody"), and the ending. Very thorough. B made Hask the seller of the core, who leaves to buy it back. It reached Orsa's want, scenes 2, 4, 5, 7, 8, the Hask/Kiri mirror, and Decided. Also good; B's guilt-for-grievance swap is a sharper reversal in spirit but the Decided deletions leave gaps.
- Rework 2: A refused the homecoming, bought a fleet, and had the Thukker arrive unasked in the climax because Haldra paid Lusse. It reached the Lusse entry, scenes 7–10, the Ending and "Still open". B moved Aino's escape a year earlier, so her freedom caused Kiri's swap. It reached Kiri's want, the events list, scenes 3 and 9, and the mirror with Hask. Both are well done. Leftover in B: the waiting-list item about why Kiri went in was not updated, and event labels were partly changed in other places. A slight edge to A for cleanliness; B's change is more thematically interesting.

**4. The scene.**
- A ("The Cradle"): the climax from inside the leash. It uses the reworks fully (crews paid and gone, "nobody had been paid to love her", the ledger, Haldra's hauler, "we are not turning round"). It has a real choice and a payoff.
- B ("The voice in the hull"): a good quiet scene. Kiri loses Aino's name and finds it when Hask steps in. Hask confesses the sale. Aino realises she ran and nobody came. It uses both reworks. It is less dramatic, with Hask's confession as exposition. The better scene is A.

**5. Has what it needs.** Both do. A is more concentrated: Saisi against Kaeli, Sev against Kaaven. B has wants and stakes for everyone, but the pressure is shared among many.

**Preference: A. Confidence: low.**
