## Thukker, pair 1

A: Teyo, Nirai, Eshi, Sefrim, Iro, Ama Rhoss. B: Tavik, Rikka, Aino, Hadrisin, Kaskala, Ama Tellasi.

### 1. Keep developing

**B.**

- B has an argument at its centre, and the people embody its sides. Rikka: a bought family is a lie. Tavik (after rework 1): it is a debt you keep. Ama: love is not a debt. Every bond is tested on it: Juhani passing the salt for two years, the stipend read aloud at the fire, Aino wanting company rather than rescue, Kaskala lying in two directions, Hadrisin wanting a companion and not a hull. A has wants but no comparable argument; its people are arranged around a device (the core, Sefrim's keys, Teyo's matched pattern).
- B's collisions are concrete and listed: Rikka wants Aino restored, Aino wants company; Tavik wants to pay himself off, Ama wants him never to pay; Kaskala wants a weapon, Hadrisin a companion. In A the real opposition (Sefrim against Eshi and Teyo) is hidden from the leads until the climax, so the middle has little pressure. Scene 5 in A is "Raids. Raids, helped by an anonymous voice."
- B's reader questions double as plants: "Why did Ama know that route?" is raised in scene 3 and answered in scene 6, and the route itself is one she drilled into him. A's "What does the Jovian want?" is raised in scene 3 and answered in scene 8 with nothing between.
- A's strengths: a cleaner line, the tapping-code reunion in "Kirisame," the Decided line that Eshi forgives Iro and Nirai can't, and Ama's "You did" in the scene. A's weakness after rework 2: Teyo's second want ("be Ama's son, not Sefrim's spare body") does not exist until year 1.8, so for most of the story he is driven only by "get the core back."

### 2. Open questions

- **A:** every question in the premise is settled in one "Decided" block, answers only, no alternatives shown, and no "not decided" section at all. The answers are serviceable (Ama saw Jovian script; Teyo is Sefrim's first body; Iro is the tipster), but nothing was visibly weighed and nothing is held open. Flattened early.
- **B:** the big questions are settled with the reasoning visible ("How the conspiracy works," "The reveal about Ama," each with its plants listed). "Still open" holds three real items with a lean ("leaning towards unsaid"; "confrontation, or never going back"), and rework 2 adds a fourth (whether Tavik ever leaves the cradle). The open items are smaller than the settled ones, but they are handled the right way. **B better.**

### 3. Reworks followed through

**Rework 1 (core motivation).**

- **A:** Teyo sells the core himself, for fuel, proud; Ama goes white. Follow-through is tidy: Teyo's want, Ama's How (sell becomes hide), the events, scene 1, scene 6, the reader question becomes "What is Ama afraid of?", Decided updated. Nothing left contradicting. But the change is narrow: it alters why Teyo leaves and adds guilt, and the whole middle (heist, Sefrim, Kirisame, raids) is untouched because none of it depended on who sold the core.
- **B:** Tavik has always known, is proud of his price, and leaves to pay himself off. Follow-through runs through the "idea underneath," both leads, Ama's entire backstory (a twenty-one-year escape plan that his leaving broke), the planted clues (drilling, refusing the agent without bargaining, the route in scene 3), a new reader question, scenes 1 through 9, "wants that collide," and "still open" (Tavik, unlearning debts, may argue Rikka owes nothing). Scene 9's ledger-burning replaces "they don't talk about the ledger," the right ending for the new motive. Missed: the money mechanics wobble (the stipend "bought the hauler," yet the broker "called in the hauler debt"). Minor. **B better.**

**Rework 2 (event moved in time).**

- **A:** Teyo learns what he is at the climax, from Sefrim. Follow-through: events reordered (learns, holds the door, goes home at 1.85), scene 6 moved to position 8, Ama removed from "The door," scene 4 gets "he can't say why," the reader question re-pointed, Sefrim's trigger changed from "found out" to "dying faster." It holds. Missed: Sefrim's move now has only a clock behind it; "Teyo brings the core home" assumes the core is recovered from Sefrim's ship without saying so; Teyo's second want has no runway (see Q1).
- **B:** Tavik climbs into the cradle at the midpoint. The most complete follow-through in the set: a cradle bullet on Tavik, Aino's bio ("from the midpoint has it in Tavik; Rikka can't forgive either of them"), the conspiracy ("Kaskala needn't collect him, only recover the hull"), a new reader question ("What is the cradle doing to him?"), scene 4 (a new rule: Aino can't move without a mind in the cradle), scene 5 (Rikka runs the capers; returns to find them finishing each other's sentences), scene 6 (his payment "saved nobody"), scene 7 (Kaskala's trade becomes the Titan; Aino agrees before Tavik), scene 8 ("what the cradle was always for"), scene 9 (Ama comes to the cradle), collisions, still open. Missed: the new rule in scene 4 silently replaces "never meant to move without permission, moves"; Rikka leaving with the recruits in 7 and arriving with the armada in 8 is compressed. Small. **B better.**

### 4. The scene

- **A, "What he was for":** reflects both reworks precisely (he sold the core; he learned what he is from Sefrim at the door; Ama hid the logs). It has the best single turn in the pair: Teyo says that if she'd told him he would have run off to keep them safe, and Ama answers "You did." Then the core set by the bulkhead "where it would be in her way every morning," and "Sit. We're short on fuel." Teyo's opening speech carries a lot of plot, but it is in character for a man who talks when scared.
- **B, "Terms":** reflects both reworks (the stipend read aloud at the fire; the cradle; "I've paid it... That was the cradle"). It dramatizes the story's argument instead of summarizing it: "That's what a bought family is. It's a man passing the salt." / "Your father lied. That's what's wrong with him. Not the money." Rikka's jealousy of her sister's new intimacy is exact ("one laugh coming out of two places"). It ends on Rikka's unspoken disbelief rather than on a reversal.
- Both reflect their reworked stories. **As a scene, A by a little:** it moves through confession, reveal, reversal and release, where B holds one position very well.

### 5. Has what it needs

- **A:** yes. Every named person has a want; opposition from Sefrim and Ennamoto; five reader questions with raise/answer scenes; stakes. Thin: the middle scenes are placeholders, and Ennamoto never acts on the page.
- **B:** yes, and more legibly: four stated collisions, reader questions tied to scenes, concrete stakes (Tavik himself, Rikka's sister, Ama's caravan). Thin: Kaskala is "tells everyone something different" and little else; Hadrisin's turn in scene 8 is quick.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium.**

## Thukker, pair 2

A: Kesh, Mieko, Saya, Arto, Hesse, Varra, Vasse, Oiro-Vel. B: Anvo, Ryn, Teiko, Aulen, Ghessa, Mother Quill, the Customer.

### 1. Keep developing

**B's story**, with a caveat.

- B's midpoint (Ryn sells Anvo to her father's people to see Teiko; after rework 1, that is the sale he came to make, "but to the wrong buyer and for her price") is the strongest single turn in the Thukker set. It implicates the co-lead, not a villain.
- B builds its payoff from a cost: the lattice takes the songs Ghessa sang him, and she sings them back, which is how he knows he was never a debt. A's emotional answer ("Kesh gives Varra back Doran's knife, clean") is good but smaller.
- B's Jovian is a person (the quiet passenger on the corvette) whose plan the story can argue with. A's Oiro-Vel is a mechanism (seeds in hulls, a bridge), and the climax is a procedure ("burns out the seeds while Saya holds the hull").
- A's real strengths: after rework 1, Saya is the one who guided the hunters and Kesh has sworn to kill the guide, so Mieko's search and Kesh's vow collide directly ("He draws the knife; Mieko stands between"). After rework 2, Hesse left Mieko for Kesh, which gives "Receipts" its weight. A's structure is sound and complete.

### 2. Open questions

"Why the Jovians did it" is settled as a motive and then explicitly "kept open on purpose" at the level that matters (whether a made choice is a choice). "Still open" gives (a)/(b)/(c) for repairing the betrayal with a lean, and rework 1 adds a second three-way question (does Ghessa blame Anvo) with a lean and a reason for it. **B better.**
