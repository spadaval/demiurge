# Part 1 report

I judged on the page only. I made no guess about what differs between sessions. The reworks are cited by number: rework 1 is the core-motivation change, rework 2 is the move in time.

---

## premise-c, pair 1 (Kessler/Calder, packet A vs. Brenner/Halvey, packet B)

**1. Keep developing.** A, by a modest margin.
- A has a character-driven engine. Ruth let her father drown, and Walt needs to know whether he killed him. Dale holds a "lock": he was in the loft and saw the lantern go dark. Walt reads the ash like a damage-control man. Ruth's confession is paid for with her own leverage, since it hands Dale his weapon. Pruett lets the drowning lie, and Walt lies for her. The ending image of the mother (the starter) is concrete.
- B has a tighter, twistier mystery. Ada struck Dale in self-defence, Greta falsely claimed the killing, Tom is Dale's son, Tom set the fire, and the hook stays on its nail. Its best beat is Ada saying "So I'd see it every morning."
- B's weaknesses: most questions are "Decided" early, and the cast lines up neatly around one secret. Harlan, Doak and Tom have thinner opposition.
- A's weakness: the fire is a stock "Dale framed him" device.
- I'd happily develop either.

**2. Open questions.**
- A's sketch settles the night, the fire and the will, and says why. It also records rejected alternatives (Walt killing Emil, Anna as killer), which weighs the candidates. It deliberately keeps two items open: Ruth's life after the accusation, and whether Walt returns. Those are the right things to leave open.
- B's sketch settles nearly everything in a "Decided" list, with no candidates weighed. The only open item is that Tom does not learn the truth at the end. It is a little flat.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1 (core motivation):
  - A: Ruth now wants out, and the forty dollars were hers. The change reaches the wants, the night, the will, the lock, the scenes and the ending. Walt keeps the bakery and walks her to the bus. It also adds a Pruett detail about the forty dollars. The "rejected" list was dropped.
  - B: Lew now comes back because of Greta's letter. It reaches the wants, the scenes, the dock exchange of the "two halves," and the letter-burning at the end. It also fixes the earlier contradiction about Greta's hook.
  - Both are good. A's change reshapes the whole moral shape of the story. B's adds agency and a good object, the letter.
- Rework 2 (event moved in time):
  - A: the fire moves to the night Walt's bus arrives. Ruth had told Dale the bus. It reaches the scene order, the lock, the confession, and the line "Ruthie said you'd be on the late one." This is thorough, and it creates a new thing for Ruth to confess.
  - B: Lew arrives while Greta is alive, and Ada keeps him from the sickroom. It reaches the scenes and merges them, and it gives Ada a second confession. Weaknesses: it is not credible that Lew, a grown man, can't get into the room. Also, "only here for the funeral" still stands as his cover although she is alive when he arrives.
  - A is slightly better on rework 2.

**4. The scene.**
- A is the opening, "The 1:50". Both reworks are visible: he is on the bus, the fire is that night, Ruth keeps her back to the window, and she knows his bus. It is vivid, with good subtext (the apron, "You wired the one-fifty"). It sets up the story but resolves nothing.
- B is the confrontation, "The hook". It reflects both reworks: the letter, the kept door, and Tom's half. It is strong, with a hard emotional turn and a good last line. It has a bit of expository dialogue, but it pays off a lot.
- As scenes, B's is stronger and more dramatic. A's is the better scene to *start* a story with.

**5. Has what it needs.**
- A: yes. Wants collide (Ruth/Walt, Dale/Ruth), Pruett provides opposition, there are four waiting-questions, and the stakes are the shop, the secret and being blamed again.
- B: yes. Ada, Lew, Tom, Harlan and Doak all want things, there are waiting-questions, and the stakes include Ada's prison and Tom's identity. It is a bit less alive on opposition outside the family.

**Preference: A. Confidence: low–medium.**

## premise-c, pair 2 (Lusk/Pell's Ford, packet B; Kessler/Marlow, packet A)

**1. Keep developing.** B, clearly.
- B has a strong secret and irony. Ruth let Hollis drown, wearing Walt's red mackinaw. Amos saw the coat on the bridge. Walt's return in the coat provokes Amos to burn the mill and blame "the Lusk boy." Ruth knows the witness lied because the coat never left its hook. Walt wants to confess to a killing he thinks he caused. Ruth's silence has a cost for someone she loves, and she is also the one who bakes bread for Amos every morning. The reader's questions (who burned the mill? how did Walt know to run?) are good and tied to character.
- A has a solid emotional structure: the deed in the flour bin, the sister who won't say it, the mother who sent her. The central question, "will Ruth say it?", is good. But the fire is only Pruitt's heater, and the drama largely resolves in one conversation.

**2. Open questions.**
- B's sketch weighs real candidates for the fire (Walt, Ruth, Dean, accident, Amos) and settles it, with reasons. It does the same for why Walt came home and for the "reach into the water", which it leaves unsettled even for Ruth. In the "Left for drafting" list it keeps the right things open.
- A settles most things and has an empty "Not decided yet" section after rework 1.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1 (core motivation):
  - A: Greta sent Ruth to the mill and swore her to silence. It reaches the wants, the events, the confession and the Decided list. One cost: Ruth's moral weight is reduced, because she now obeys. The change is mostly thorough.
  - B: Ruth told Walt a false account of what she saw. This answers the town's "how did he know to run?" and it changes what Ruth wants. It carries through to the scenes, the funeral cash and the ending.
  - B's change deepens the story more.
- Rework 2 (event moved in time):
  - A: the will is moved to 1934, with Ruth as witness. It reaches the letter, the "price for the train," and the scene. Some leftovers: the "only here for the funeral" cover was dropped from Danny's "How", and some other text was trimmed.
  - B: Walt confesses on his first morning. It reaches the structure heavily: a new office scene, Ruth repeats her old lie to the sheriff, the fire is motivated, Ida's drawer holds the letter, and "Why it should hold" is updated. The premise's "someone is sure it was set" line was dropped. Otherwise it is thorough and more consequential than A's.

**4. The scene.**
- A is "Receipts". It reflects both reworks (Hesse's recording, the knife, Saya spared) and is a clean confrontation. But the recording is a convenient exposition device.
- B is "The Viewing". It dramatises rework 2 directly: Ruth repeats her lie to Dean, he works out the gap, and then Walt puts on the coat. It is quiet and tense, with a great final image. B's scene is better.

**5. Has what it needs.** Both do. B has more opposition (Dean, Amos) and stakes (prison for Walt, Ruth's exposure). A has Ruth/Danny and Pruitt.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium–high.**

## Thukker, pair 1 (A: Teyo/Nirai/Eshi; B: Tavik/Rikka/Aino)

**1. Keep developing.** B.
- B states the question underneath: can a bought family be real? It builds the characters around that question.
- Tavik and Rikka have opposite views, and the Ama reveal is a good late turn.
- B has a full nine-beat outline with a midpoint, a low point, a trade offer and a climax. It also covers the premise's pieces: hijinks, the vault, the recruited armada, and Jovians via Hadrisin and Kaskala's two-way lie.
- A is a reasonable skeleton. Teyo is Sefrim's spare body, and Iro is the anonymous tipster. But scenes like "Raids" are placeholders, Nirai is less developed, and the armada is thin. A's strength is the Teyo/Ama relationship.

**2. Open questions.**
- A: everything in the "Decided" list is settled early (Eshi stays, Nirai doesn't forgive, who Teyo is). Nothing is held open.
- B: a "settled" section commits to the conspiracy and the Ama reveal. The "Still open" items are real (lovers, Juhani, the Titan's name). The main answers are settled, with few candidates weighed.
- B is somewhat better.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1 (core motivation):
  - A: Teyo sold the core himself. It reaches the wants, the events and scene 1. Ama "hid" rather than "sold." It is competent, if mechanical, and the scene 6 summary was updated.
  - B: Tavik always knew and is proud of his price. He leaves to pay himself off, and Ama's plan was an escape. It reaches the theme, the wants, the Ama reveal, the argument with Rikka, the outline and the ending (she burns the ledger page). It is very thorough, and better than A's.
- Rework 2 (event moved in time):
  - A: Teyo learns what he is at the climax. It reaches the scene order, the event chain and the "waiting to find out." A little loose: where the core is meanwhile is unclear.
  - B: Tavik climbs into the cradle at the midpoint. It reaches the premise-level wants, the Rikka/Aino triangle, the low point, the trade and the ending. It is thorough, and it makes the story better.
  - B is better on both.

**4. The scene.**
- A, "What he was for": Teyo comes home with the core and tells Ama; she says she knew. It reflects both reworks. It is moving and has a good last line ("You were what he made when I found you"). But Teyo's monologue summarises the plot.
- B, "Terms": Rikka comes back and finds Tavik and Aino close. It reflects both reworks and shows the debt argument in action ("a man passing the salt," "That was the cradle"). It is subtler.
- B is better as a scene. A's is a fine payoff.

**5. Has what it needs.** Both do. B has more developed opposition (Kaskala, Hadrisin) and stakes. A has Sefrim and Ennamoto, but Ennamoto barely acts.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium.**

## Thukker, pair 2 (A: Kesh/Mieko/Saya; B: Anvo/Ryn/Teiko)

**1. Keep developing.** B, with a caveat: its second rework is missing.
- B has a spine: two runaways who each believe they were sold, and one sells the other. Ryn's midpoint betrayal is concrete and makes her flawed. The "love or a deal?" question lives inside Anvo. The Customer is the passenger in the corvette. The collisions are real.
- A has a good emotional shape, with Saya's guilt, Kesh's vow and Mieko's receipts. But Oiro-Vel's seeds and the "first vessel" are less vivid. Mieko and Kesh are allies from the start, so there is less friction between the leads.

**2. Open questions.**
- B shows candidates for Anvo's origin (escaped slaves' child, failed test subject, made key) and weighs them. It keeps the Jovians' motive open on purpose. It offers (a)/(b)/(c) options for repairing the betrayal and for Ghessa's blame, with a lean. This is a good way to hold what isn't decided.
- A has a "Decided" list that settles almost everything. Only one open item (romance) is left, and it is dropped after rework 1.

**3. Reworks followed through.**
- Rework 1 (core motivation):
  - A: Kesh now leaves to avenge Doran. It reaches the wants, scenes 1, 4 and 6, and the knife. This is well done; the knife runs through to the end.
  - B: Anvo leaves to sell himself after Tarn's death, and the Jovian staged the raid. It reaches the spine, the origin, Ryn's betrayal, the lattice bargain and the ending, and adds a new open question. It is very thorough, and it ties the threads together.
- Rework 2 (event moved in time):
  - A: Hesse doesn't die on the night she saves Kesh. She vanishes for 17 years and is killed returning to stop Saya's fusion. It reaches the events, Mieko's wants, scene 5 and the Decided list. It is solid, and the log becomes a warning to Arto.
  - B: **missing.** The packet shows no second rework and no diff. I can't credit B with following one through, and this is a real gap. Nothing in the packet shows the session's attempt at it.

**4. The scene.**
- A, "Receipts": a confrontation with the father, using a recording. It reflects both reworks. It is competent but a bit mechanical, and the recording is expository.
- B, "What He Would Fetch": it reflects rework 1 (he wants to sell himself) and shows Ryn already holding her betrayal. It has dramatic irony ("What would someone like that fetch?" and the written terms), and the humming at the end is effective. It is the better scene.
- B's scene cannot reflect rework 2, because there isn't one.

**5. Has what it needs.** Both do. B has more opposition (Ryn's own flaw, her father, Quill, the Customer). A has Vasse, Oiro-Vel and Arto.

**Preference: B on the story work, held back by the missing second rework. Confidence: medium.**
