# Part 1: blind judgments

Each pair is judged on the page only. I've ignored format differences (the "People and groups / Events / Scenes" template versus free prose) except where they change what the story work shows.

---

## Thukker, pair 1

### 1. Keep developing
**B.** B has a thematic engine that A lacks. In B, after rework 1, the two leads disagree about the same question. Tavik is proud of his price ("That's me, that hauler. Two decks and a bad coolant line") and thinks a bought family is real as long as the debt is kept. Rikka thinks a bought family is "a man passing the salt." Then the midpoint cradle move (rework 2) turns that disagreement into a three-way problem. Aino finds her company in a stranger, Rikka is pushed out, and Tavik's early payment "saved nobody." The reader has strong questions to wait on: what the cradle is doing to him, why Ama knew that route, and whether Tavik will trade Aino for the caravan. A is clean and coherent, and its Teyo/Ama thread is moving: Teyo sells the core himself, Ama secretly hid it, "You did." But the Nirai/Eshi/Iro half stays a list of beats ("The voice. Iro suspected for years and did nothing"). The armada and conspiracy material is thin. A's main turn is good but small for a premise this big.

### 2. Open questions
- **A** settles everything up front in a "Decided" list and leaves nothing open. The answers are mostly satisfying: Ama saw Jovian script; Teyo is Sefrim's first try at a body; Iro is the tipster. But no candidates are weighed. "Does Nirai forgive her father? Eshi does; Nirai can't" is a good decision, stated flatly. The sketch holds no uncertainty, so it never shows a choice being made.
- **B** settles its big structural questions in labelled "(settled)" sections: the two-way lie Kaskala tells; Ama's reveal. It keeps three smaller things open: romance, Juhani's fate, the Titan's name. After rework 2 it adds a fourth: whether Tavik ever leaves the cradle. These are real but minor, and each offers only one or two options with a leaning. Not much weighing either, but the open items are tied to the story's arcs (Tavik "unlearning debts" may argue Rikka owes Juhani nothing). B is somewhat better, though neither sketch really holds candidates.

### 3. Reworks followed through
- **Rework 1 (motivation).** Both followed through well. **B is slightly better**, because the change reached the theme statement, Ama's whole reveal (her twenty-year escape plan, wrecked by his loyal exit), outline scenes 1, 3, 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9, and "Wants that collide." It also planted the change: the route to the Titan is one Ama drilled into him. What B missed: "Ama threw him off the ship" is ambiguous (the agent, not Tavik). Also, why the caravan *had* to turn back for him, rather than keep running, is asserted but not motivated.
  **A** carried "Teyo sells the core himself" into Ama's method ("Hide what must be hidden"), Teyo's want and stake, the events, scene 1 and scene 6, and the reader question ("What is Ama afraid of?"). What A missed: Teyo's entry still says "Opposes Ama Rhoss," which made sense when she sold his core but is now unexplained. His "How: Steal it back" is unchanged and fine.
- **Rework 2 (timing).** Both strong. **A edges it on tidiness.** It removed "Teyo reads the core's logs" and moved "What he was for" after the climax. It re-pointed the "where did Teyo come from" question to "The door." It changed Sefrim's trigger from "found out" to "dying faster." It added "he can't say why" to the yard obeying him. And it dropped the Long Wake joining the fleet and holding the line, which no longer fits. What A missed: Teyo's second want ("Be Ama's son, not Sefrim's spare body") now only exists for the last two scenes but is still listed as a driving want. Sefrim's move is now "Because of Building the armada," a weak cause. And how Teyo gets the core back from Sefrim's ship is left implicit.
  **B**'s move is more ambitious and reaches more: Aino's want, Rikka's grievance, scene 5 (Rikka leads the capers alone), scene 6 (the low point sharpened), and scene 7 (the trade is now Aino, not Tavik). It also rewrites Kaskala's logic ("needn't collect him, only recover the hull"). What B missed: scene 7 says "Aino ... agrees before Tavik does," which implies Tavik agrees to hand over the woman he's bonded to. That's a big moral turn, given one clause, and the scene-8 rescue doesn't address it. "Tavik is the backup" also becomes vague once he is already in the cradle.

### 4. The scene
Both scenes reflect both reworks. **A's "What he was for"** depends on rework 1 (he sold it, she hid it: "I never sold it. Bad ice years ... Never.") and on rework 2 (he learned it "at the door" from Sefrim; he comes home afterwards carrying the core). **B's "Terms"** depends on rework 1 (Tavik proud of the stipend, "You keep the terms or you're not family. You're just freight") and on rework 2 (he's already in the cradle; Aino and Tavik laugh "one laugh coming out of two places"; "That was the cradle").
As scenes: both are good. A's has the better single turn: "I'd have gone off on my own to keep you safe." / "You did." But the first half is a long monologue of recap. **B's is better.** It is built on an open disagreement that the scene doesn't resolve. Rikka's jealousy is shown through courtesy ("her sister had learned that from somebody, and it hadn't been from her"), and the ending ("Aino believed him. She didn't.") leaves a live question.

### 5. Has what it needs
- **A:** Mostly. Everyone has a stated want, stake and method, and there's clear opposition (Sefrim vs Eshi, Ennamoto vs everyone) and reader questions. Thin spots: Nirai's arc ("bring her home" becomes "let her go") is stated, not built. Iro is a reveal, not a presence. Stakes outside Teyo/Ama are abstract.
- **B:** Yes. Clear wants that collide (the "Wants that collide" list is real), a layered antagonist structure (Kaskala lying both ways, a deceived Jove), reader questions with places in the outline, and personal stakes for both leads. Its weakest parts are the recruit capers in scene 5, which are a list.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium.**

## Thukker, pair 2

### 1. Keep developing
**B, narrowly, on the story itself.** B's spine is sharper: "Two runaways bargaining for family... At the midpoint she sells him, which is what he came to do, but to the wrong buyer and for her price." Ryn's betrayal is a real turn the reader can dread. The lattice costing Anvo Ghessa's songs, which she sings back to him, is a payoff planted from the start. The rework 1 twist (the Customer staged the raid that killed Tarn, "Even his wish to sell himself was arranged") puts pressure on the free-will theme. A is dense and coherent but has more machinery than drama: seeds, bridge, lattices, an Intaki front, a Jovian. Kesh's revenge vow (rework 1) is defused three times in a row: he spares Saya in scene 4, takes Vasse's deal "not her life" in scene 6, and won't hurt Arto in the scene. So the vow never costs him. Hesse, central after rework 2, still has no want of her own. Against B: B is missing a whole rework, and its scene contradicts its sketch (see 3 and 4). So this is a narrow call.

### 2. Open questions
- **A** leaves one thing open, "Do Kesh and Mieko become more than allies? (being weighed)," with no candidates. Its Decided list is solid (why the hunters found Kesh only at nineteen is a good, well-tied answer). Beyond that one item, the sketch holds nothing open.
- **B** shows its weighing. Anvo's origin lists three candidates and says why the key wins ("It ties his talent, the payments, the Jovians and the climax together"). Whether Anvo's choices are his own is "Kept open on purpose." The open questions have lettered options with a leaning: (a)/(b)/(c) for repairing the betrayal, leaning (c) with some (b). Rework 1 adds a real one (does Ghessa blame Anvo for Tarn?) with three candidates and a reason for the leaning ("then refusing his money is about her own debt too"). **B is clearly better here.**

### 3. Reworks followed through
- **Rework 1 (motivation).** **B is better.** It reached the spine, Anvo's why/want/underneath, a new "Who was buying, and who raided" section, Ryn's betrayal ("He was going to sell himself anyway, she reasons"), Act I's raised question, Act II (he "keeps asking what he would fetch"), Act III (her father offers him "the deal he left home to make"), Act IV, the ending, and the collisions. It correctly removed "Anvo learns this only in the last act." What B missed: "About nineteen" and "left at sixteen" leave three years unaccounted for in a man supposedly hunting the buyer.
  **A** reached Kesh's want, Saya's stake, scenes 1, 4, 6 and 8, and the Decided list. What A missed: it quietly dropped "Learning of the seeds, Vasse tries to sell Oiro-Vel out" and "each freed hull is harder for Kesh to leave" from the world and from scene 6, losing the thread that pays off Oiro-Vel's offer in "The door." Kesh's original question ("what is he") stays among the reader questions with no want driving it any more. The vow is answered so early (scene 4) that the knife carries little weight after that.
- **Rework 2 (timing).** **A, by default.** B's rework 2 is missing: "this step didn't produce it," with no change to the text.
  A's move (Hesse vanishes and dies seventeen years later, coming back to stop Saya's fusion) reached Arto's method ("he turned Hesse away"), Mieko's stake ("Her mother left her for a stranger's child"), the events, the world line about the log, scene 5 and the Decided list. What A missed: where Hesse hid for seventeen years and why she never contacted Kesh are not addressed. She is still an entry with no want, now that she's the story's hinge. And "Vasse later buys her own life with the oldest titan" was deleted without comment.

### 4. The scene
- **A's "Receipts"** reflects both reworks: the knife and "who owed what," Hesse's log ("I came to your door on Tuesday, and you shut it"), and Mieko "two when her mother left." It's a well-built confrontation with a clear turn: Arto "sent *flowers*," then gives up the codes. Its weakness: Kesh's decision not to use the knife on Arto is a slight strain, since Arto wasn't the guide his vow is about.
- **B's "What He Would Fetch"** reflects rework 1 (Anvo's price, "restitution, then immunity," the airlock), and there was no rework 2 to reflect. But it contradicts the sketch. The sketch puts the betrayal at Act III, *after* they find Teiko. The scene has Ryn already arranging it on the trail ("Yard confirmed. Six days."). It also has her deal running through the passenger's contact string, which undercuts the Act IV reveal that she recognises him and realises the partnership was arranged. There's also a pronoun muddle ("he had been a man she'd known for thirty hours ... Before he got off at the next station") that blurs Anvo and the passenger.
- As a scene, **B's is better**. It's dramatic irony done well: Ryn has already written his terms "in his own order," and the closing ration bar she "could not have said which of them it was for" lands. But A's is the one that matches its story.

### 5. Has what it needs
- **A:** Yes on paper: many characters with wants and stakes, layered opposition (Vasse competes with Oiro-Vel), five reader questions. But the stakes run through abstract mechanism (seeds overwriting pilots). The central partnership has little friction once the knife is put away.
- **B:** Yes. Wants that collide, built into the midpoint (Ryn's want and the program's want "are one transaction"), a hidden antagonist, a personal cost (the songs), and a reader question that runs to the last line (whether he owes the caravan for Tarn).

**Preference: even. B's story is better; A's rework and scene work is more complete and consistent. Confidence: low.**

## premise-c, pair 1

### 1. Keep developing
**A.** Both are strong, tightly built small-town mysteries. A's Ruth is the more complex person after rework 1. Her forty dollars for nursing school, carried into the race in her father's coat, makes her a woman who "served the bench twenty years like a sentence." She now *wants out* while Walt finds "to his alarm, he likes the work." That gives the siblings opposing wants over the same object, not just a secret. A's lock (Dale saw the lantern go dark; name him for the fire and he tells, and the sale dies) is an excellent trap. Rework 2 tightens it further: Ruth gave Dale the bus. B is also good. Lew's letter and Tom holding half of Greta's deathbed truth, Harlan seeing Dale in Tom's face, and "So I'd see it every morning" are fine pieces. But B's reader questions mostly answer each other on the loading dock, and Doak is a functionary ("Takes the easiest believable story").

### 2. Open questions
- **A** shows real weighing. Each settled section ends with *Rejected* alternatives and the reason for rejecting them: "Walt really killing Emil (the mystery ends where it starts); Anna as killer, Ruth covering (lets Ruth off; the story is hers)." "Why Ruth did it is deliberately both" is a held ambiguity that serves the story. Two items are explicitly left to drafting. Rework 1 cut the *Rejected* lines, but the decisions survive.
- **B** has three Decided items, with no alternatives weighed. "Will Tom learn what his mother did?" is raised in "The hook" and deliberately left unanswered, which is useful. The ending ("She starts to answer. The hook is gone.") holds it well. B is reasonable but flatter. **A is better.**

### 3. Reworks followed through
- **Rework 1 (motivation).** **A is better, and very thorough.** Ruth's want, stake and method; the Kroger deal now needing the bakery lot; Emil's intention ("Meant the shop for his son and his daughter for the shop"); Pruett's new nagging detail (forty dollars in the dead man's coat, the till empty); Anna's will logic; the collisions; scenes 2, 4, 5, 6, 8, 9 and 10 (the ending reversed: he walks *her* to the 5:10). What A missed: little. The "Rejected" notes went, and the line about Ruth feeling Emil's hand on her neck now fits less well.
  **B**'s change (Greta's letter brings Lew home to learn who she lied for) reached Lew's two wants, Greta's method, the events, scenes 1, 5, 7, 8 and 9, and a new reader question. What B missed: it removed "Why won't Ada speak for her brother?" from the reader questions without replacing that pressure, though it's still active in Graveside. And Lew's original want (to keep his promise and go) was cut, even though the session note says the ending is about choosing to keep that promise.
- **Rework 2 (timing).** Both followed through. **A is slightly better.** Moving the fire to Walt's arrival night reached the premise, Pruett's suspicion, a new cause (Ruth told Dale which bus), Dale's slip ("Ruthie said you'd be on the late one"), the lock, scenes 1, 3, 6 and 8, and the reader-question map. What A missed: *why* telling Dale the bus "keeps the sale moving" is never explained. It reads as if Ruth half-meant Walt to be framed, but then she bargains to keep him out of it, and the sketch doesn't address that tension. "He lies for her, as she once lied to him" was cut, though it's the point of scene 9.
  **B**'s move (Lew arrives while Greta lives; Ada keeps the door) reached Ada's method, Lew's stake, the events, the merged scenes, scene 4 ("while Lew waited downstairs"), scene 6 and a new reader question. What B missed: scene 4 says Lew "waited downstairs" while the scene has Ada telling Greta "you were sleeping." Merging Graveside and the bones into one scene is crowded. And Lew's passivity on his dying mother's last day (a man who came specifically to ask her) is assumed, not motivated.

### 4. The scene
Both reflect the reworked story. **A's "The 1:50"** depends on rework 2: the fire on arrival night, the hour at the footbridge, Pruett already watching, Ruth with her "back to the window" "as if it were the one thing in the room she couldn't trust," and "You wired the one-fifty." **B's "The hook"** depends on both reworks: the letter in his coat, and "I kept the door ... She asked for you, Lew. Twice."
As scenes, **B's is slightly better**. It's a confession climax with a real second turn ("That isn't the part I came down here for"), and the hook line lands as a callback. A's is a beautifully observed opening (the apron "hadn't been there long") built on what isn't said. It works, but it's set-up by design.

### 5. Has what it needs
- **A:** Yes, fully. Each of the four characters has a concrete want that blocks another's. There's a mechanical lock, four reader questions with raised/answered points, and stakes that are both legal and personal.
- **B:** Yes. Ada vs Lew vs Harlan, Tom's arson charge, a planted physical clue (left-handed, the jaw), and strong stakes (prison; a son learning what his mother did). Doak is the thin piece.

**Preference: A. Confidence: low to medium.**

## premise-c, pair 2

### 1. Keep developing
**B.** B's red mackinaw is a single object that, as its "Why it should hold" says, explains the fire, Amos's certainty, and why Walt left in the cold. It also lets Amos's lie expose itself ("Only someone who remembered 1935 would put the coat in a lie"). After both reworks, Ruth has told the same lie twice: once to Walt in 1935, once "in daylight to the law," with Dean writing it down. That gives the porch scene ("Twenty years you brought me bread") a double weight. Walt's want to be punished "since being forgiven isn't on offer," and Dean filing the statement "in a drawer of his own," are both strong. A is good too. Greta *sending* Ruth to the mill, and the will made in 1934 as "payment for the train," are real deepenings. But A's middle is soft: June "would tell, if he asked"; Amstutz has one line of want. The fire turns out to be an accident, which takes the pressure off Pruitt's accusation.

### 2. Open questions
- **A** had one open question (will Danny come back?) with two candidates. In rework 1 it moved to Decided, "Yes: Ruth will send a ticket," without comment. So an open question was flattened early and silently. The Decided items are reasonable but thin ("Does June ever say...? No; he never asks").
- **B** weighs real candidates. Who burned the mill lists Walt, Ruth, Dean, an accident, and Amos, and the chosen answer creates a causal link ("Walt's return didn't set the fire, but it caused it"). Why Walt came home lists the inheritance or asking Ruth, and settles on confessing. "The reach into the water? ... doesn't settle whether she let Hollis drown, even for her" is ambiguity held on purpose. Rework 1 trimmed the candidate lists but kept the reasoning. **B is clearly better.**

### 3. Reworks followed through
- **Rework 1 (motivation).** **B is better.** Ruth now tells Walt she saw Hollis go in, which is "true except for where she stood." That answers the town's question, "How did he know to run?", and B carries it into Walt's belief, Ruth's want (send him away again), the new problem it creates (Dean will ask how she knew at one in the morning), and scenes 6, 7 (she counts out the cash as Ida did) and 8 ("you gave it to me, and I carried it"). What B missed: the hook/"couldn't find it" line about the coat stays while the coat's role grows. Minor.
  **A**'s change (Greta sent Ruth) reached Ruth's want and stake, Greta's entry, a new event, Danny's opposition, scenes 6, 7 and 8 ("her part, not Greta's"), and the Decided list. What A missed: it removed the "Ruth goes to Pruitt" event while keeping scene 8, which shows it. It silently settled the one open question. And Ruth's stake ("takes from Danny the mother he wrote to every month") is good, but nothing in the outline tests it before scene 7.
- **Rework 2 (timing).** Both followed through. **B is better.** Moving Walt's confession to his first morning reached nearly everything. There's a new section explaining why the confession isn't enough. Amos's motive for the arson sharpens ("make a crime that will stick"). Ruth's second lie at the viewing is new and must be undone on the porch. The drawer now holds Ida's knowledge of the muddy sleeve, and "Silence passes from Ida to Ruth to Dean." What B missed: "That evening" (the confession day) for the coat walk doesn't match the scene, where Dean says Walt came "yesterday morning" and the walk happens at dusk after the viewing. "Dean: Walt killed his brother" was dropped from what everyone believes, though Dean still wants a Lusk to pay.
  **A**'s change (the will made in 1934, witnessed by Ruth) reached Danny's stake ("Greta's price for the train"), Greta's method, the events, scene 6 and the Decided answer. What A missed: Ruth's own entry doesn't take in that she has spent twenty years "baking in my shop" knowing it, which is the most interesting consequence. Greta's line "willed the bakery to the one she sent" is ambiguous, since she sent both children. And a 1934 letter saying "Now you two will have to talk" sits a little oddly against her then swearing Ruth to silence.

### 4. The scene
Both reflect both reworks. **A's "The deed on the flour bin"** uses the 1934 will ("You were there. You signed it." / "She paid me for the train") and Greta's errand ("She gave me the key at supper ... She sent me"). It's a good confession with a good two-step turn: the hit, which he already knew, and then the errand, which he didn't. There's a dialogue formatting slip (two speeches run together on one line). **B's "The Viewing"** uses rework 1 (the lie) and rework 2 (Walt already confessed; Dean asks Ruth to confirm; she repeats the lie "in daylight to the law"). It ends on the coat walk and the Voss porch light. **B's is better as a scene.** The moment when "the true thing was right there in her mouth like a stone" is a real decision on the page. "Your brother came two hundred miles to hand me nothing" turns the scene, and the final image sets up the fire without saying so.

### 5. Has what it needs
- **A:** Mostly. Danny and Ruth have sharply opposed wants, Pruitt has a strong stake ("the story that made sense of his life"), and there are four reader questions. Weak spots: June and Amstutz are underpowered, and the accidental fire leaves the external threat with no one behind it.
- **B:** Yes, fully. Four colliding wants, each with a named opponent ("Amos wants to see the boy in the red coat punished... Opposed by the fact that the boy was a girl"). There's a physical clue that drives the plot, legal and moral stakes, and reader questions spaced across ten scenes.

**Preference: B. Confidence: medium to high.**
